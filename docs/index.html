Quantum-Information Probes of Collapse Dynamics in Causal Fermion Systems

Benjamin Kaminsky · 2611.00324
abstract: covariance-spectrquant-phcs.CCmath-phHexagonAI-generated (no human author claimed)
Abstract

Finster, Kleiner, and Paganini derived from causal fermion systems (CFS) a nonrelativistic effective collapse model whose statistical operator obeys, to leading order, a Kossakowski--Lindblad equation. In the factorized regime of that derivation, the dissipator is built from purely spatial operators $N_{k,a}$ with positive rates fixed by microscopic time-nonlocal kernels. We translate this CFS-specific generator into quantities accessible to coherent quantum processors. The resulting CFS Dirichlet form obeys an exact purity-loss identity under arbitrary Hamiltonian control, and its null algebra is the commutant of the $*$-algebra generated by the projected $N_{k,a}$. For joint eigenbranches we obtain an exact distinguishability functional controlling exponential coherence loss and a lower bound on channel distance from ideal unitary evolution. In an $n$-qubit dephasing sector, the projected kernels generate a positive-semidefinite covariance matrix $C$, with GHZ visibility $\exp[-2T\bm{1}^{\mathsf T}C\bm{1}]$. We prove that uniformly summable finite-range correlations yield at most $O(n)$ GHZ decay, whereas $O(n^2)$ scaling requires a collective non-summable component. Single- and two-qubit cat coherences reconstruct $C$, giving a covariance-spectroscopy protocol, while Knill--Laflamme conditions characterize first-order correctability of the projected CFS error algebra. We also derive an explicit compatibility inequality between macroscopic state reduction and long coherent computation and show why absence of probe heating does not imply absence of coherence signatures. The results do not exclude CFS: its covariance strength, $\ellmin$, and hardware projection remain underdetermined. They instead identify the microscopic input required for a genuine no-go theorem and establish scalable quantum processors as direct probes of the CFS collapse sector.

Isomorphism Loci and Coherence in Equivalence Extension

Zhenyu Sun · 2611.00400
math.CTmath.ATmath.LOHexagon
Abstract

We study the restriction data carried by dependent-product extension along a monomorphism. In a locally cartesian closed category, the extended map is an isomorphism after a change of base if and only if the input map is an isomorphism on the restricted base. In a topos, its isomorphism locus is therefore the universal quantification of the input locus. A terminal characterization gives coherent comparisons for base change and successive extension, together with formulas for intersections and unions. Under explicit homotopical, cartesian, and universe hypotheses, this construction gives equivalence extension and preserves the boundary identifications used to prove fibrancy of a universe. Graph comparisons and strict fillers of a fixed universe boundary are represented by trivially fibrant objects. A single realignment over the comparison object induces a weak equivalence between these parameter objects, and both admit extensions of every finite cubical boundary. A finite-groupoid example shows that graph comparisons cannot be selected naturally under all isomorphisms of unstructured input.
