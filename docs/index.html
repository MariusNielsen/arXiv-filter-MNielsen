Frobenius and perfection in framed motives

Grigory Garkusha · 2611.18212
abstract: ring spectrmath.AGmath.AT
Abstract

We develop explicit Frobenius and perfection arguments for framed motives over imperfect fields. In positive characteristic, power framing homotopies and local framed transfers make relative Frobenius invertible after inverting the characteristic. Descent through successive Frobenius twists then gives a proof of strict homotopy invariance for homotopy invariant quasi-stable additive framed presheaves and identifies the canonical comparison with the perfect closure as a stable Nisnevich local equivalence of framed motives. We extend the comparison to E-framed motives for every motivic Thom spectrum E. It also yields the cancellation theorem and the cone theorem. For commutative symmetric Thom ring spectra, we identify triangulated categories of effective E-framed motives and effective E-modules and prove perfection invariance of these triangulated categories. All comparisons are made after inverting the exponential characteristic.

Towards invertibility of derived categories of sheaf-theoretic line operators

Claudia Scheimbauer, Jackson Van Dyke · 2611.18555
abstract: infinity-categormath.QAmath-phmath.AGmath.CTcross
Abstract

We investigate when the braided monoidal $\infty$-categories of sheaf-theoretic line operators of $3$-dimensional topological quantum field theories are invertible in the higher Morita category, so that they determine invertible $4$-dimensional anomaly theories. We consider a zoo of different examples which are inherently derived. First, enhancing Ben-Zvi--Francis--Nadler, we show that a symmetric monoidal category of quasi-coherent sheaves $\mathrm{QC}(X)$ is invertible only if $X$ is a point, and convolution categories on loop stacks are Morita trivial whenever the stack satisfies descent along its diagonal, e.g. for classifying stacks. Secondly, we provide an example showing that, unlike for finite braided tensor categories, non-degeneracy does not imply invertibility in the derived setting: For an odd vector space $V[1]$, the $2$-shifted $\mathcal{D}$-modules $\mathrm{DMod}^2(V[1])$ are $3$-dualizable and non-degenerate but neither factorizable nor cofactorizable. This category is closely related to the line operators of linear Rozansky--Witten theory. Finally, we define an anomaly of a group action on an invertible braided monoidal $\infty$-category as a Witt class over $\mathrm{Rep}(G)$, and show that the category of representations of the quantum group of a torus at a root of unity is invertible relative to its quantum Frobenius.

Freeness of crossed modules, augmented quandles, and augmented racks

Toshiyuki Akita, Sota Takase · 2611.17557
math.GTmath.ATmath.GRcross
Abstract

We study freeness of crossed modules, augmented quandles, and augmented racks. We show that the associated quandle functor and the associated crossed module functor are left adjoint to the natural forgetful functors. For a map $f\colon S\to G$, we define the free augmented rack $R_f\to G$ and the free augmented quandle $Q_f\to G$. If $C_f\to G$ denotes the free crossed module determined by $f$, then the associated crossed modules of $R_f$ and $Q_f$ are both isomorphic to $C_f\to G$. We also characterize free augmented quandles in terms of stabilizers. As applications, we prove that the canonical inner augmentation of every generalized Alexander quandle is free and that the canonical inner augmentation of a classical knot quandle is free if and only if the knot is a torus knot, whereas for every $n$-knot with $n\geq2$ it is free.

Perfect Matching Complexes of $3\times 2n$ Grid Graph

Himanshu Chandrakar, Trung Chau, Shuchita Goyal, Rahul Karki, B. Subhash · 2611.18058
math.COmath.ATcross
Abstract

The perfect matching complex of a simple graph $G$ is an abstract simplicial complex whose facets (maximal faces) are precisely the perfect matchings of $G$. In this article, we study the topology of the perfect matching complex of the $3\times 2n$ grid graph and show that it is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of $f_{n-1}$ copies of $(n-1)$-dimensional spheres, where $f_{k}$ denotes the $k$-th Fibonacci number. This proves a particular case of the conjecture given by Chandrakar and Singh for perfect matching complex of grid graphs.

Stability theorems for $\varepsilon$-simplicial sets

Dominik Lachman · 2611.18195
math.CTmath.AT
Abstract

We establish stability results for internal homs of $\varepsilon$-simplicial sets, a framework motivated by nerves of Frobenius monoids in the category of relations. Rotations are modelled through edge witnesses and horn-filling conditions, without requiring unique fillers. For $\varepsilon$-fibrant objects $X$ and $Y$, we prove that the internal hom $\mathrm{Fun}(X,Y)$ satisfies the $\varepsilon$-coherence conditions and admits fillers for all $\varepsilon$-horns of dimension at least two. Its subobject consisting of simplices whose vertices correspond to $\eta$-edge-preserving maps is $\varepsilon$-fibrant. We also introduce $\eta\varepsilon$-simplicial sets by adding explicit $\eta$-witnesses and prove that the internal hom of two $\eta\varepsilon$-fibrant objects is again $\eta\varepsilon$-fibrant. Our proofs use adjunctions relating $\varepsilon$-simplicial sets and paracyclic sets. We characterise the $\varepsilon$-horn filling property by requiring the corresponding counit to be a trivial Kan fibration. Consequently, for every object satisfying this property, coherent choices of rotations of each simplex form a contractible Kan complex.
