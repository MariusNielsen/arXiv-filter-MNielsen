Lax descent and foliated Verdier duality

Tobias Dyckerhoff, Till Heine, Simon Schneider, Kostiantyn Tolmachov · 2610.29977
title: descentabstract: higher categormath.AGmath.CTmath.RT
Abstract

Higher categorical methods allow us to view recollements as lax descent data and build a corresponding descent theory governed by an $\infty$-bicategorical theory of limits. We demonstrate some concrete innovations brought along by these new techniques in the classical context of constructible and perverse sheaves on stratified spaces. Our first application uses partially (op)lax descent to recover the famous result of Beilinson telling us how to glue perverse sheaves in terms of vanishing and nearby cycles. The remainder of the article is concerened with establishing a uniform view on a series of classification results for perverse sheaves culminating in recent work of Kapranov-Schechtman. Namely, we use lax descent to establish a ``foliated'' variant of Verdier duality which, informally speaking, identifies the derived constructible category of a suitably foliated space with sections of a categorical cosheaf on the leaf space whose stalks are the categories of sheaves along the leaves. Again, this is obtained as an application of lax descent, and seems to provide a reasonable framework to formalize the idea of ``mixed sheaf-cosheaf objects'' promoted by Kapranov and Schechtman.

Pontryagin-Thom, $\pi_0$ of the motivic sphere, and principal quadratic divisors

Kyle Ormsby · 2610.29065
title: motivic spheretitle: motivicmath.ATmath.AG
Abstract

Segal computed $\pi_0$ of the equivariant sphere spectrum as the Burnside ring by a Pontryagin-Thom construction, transversality, and marks. We carry out the same argument in the motivic setting over a field $k$: the Burnside ring is replaced by the Grothendieck ring $A^u(\Gamma_k)$ of finite \'etale $k$-schemes decorated with a unit, the marks by the Dress map $d(L,u)=\mathrm{tr}_{L/k}\langle u\rangle$ into $\mathrm{GW}(k)$, and a Pontryagin-Thom map $A^u(\Gamma_k)\to\pi_0(\mathbb{S}_k)(k)$ is shown to be surjective with kernel $\ker(d)$. This gives $\pi_0(\mathbb{S}_k)(k)\cong\mathrm{GW}(k)$ for infinite perfect fields of characteristic $\ne 2$ without Morel's computation. We then identify $\ker(d)$, by a reciprocity law on $\mathbb{P}^1$, as the group of principal quadratic divisors of rational functions, so that $\pi_0$ of the motivic sphere is the unit-decorated Burnside ring of $\Gamma_k$ modulo principal quadratic divisors. The transversality step is proved by an elementary "re-topping" argument.

The moduli space of maps from hypersurfaces and its Goodwillie tower

Alexis Aumonier · 2610.28809
title: goodwilliemath.AT
Abstract

We define a moduli space of continuous maps from smooth hypersurfaces varying in an ample linear system. To study its topology, we construct a scanning map to an appropriate section space and investigate its effect on homology. Our main innovation is a general argument based on Goodwillie calculus and applicable to various moduli spaces of maps. Along the way, we compute the Goodwillie tower of the section space functor based at any retractive space.

Logic vs doctrines, a mirror symmetry

Ivan Di Liberti, Umberto Tarantino, Lingyuan Ye · 2610.29771
abstract: topoimath.CTcs.LOmath.LO
Abstract

This paper studies doctrines in categorical logic and their relations to fragments of geometric logic. After a brief account of the historical context for the notion of doctrine in categorical logic, we propose our working definition. We then show that there is a Galois connection between the poset of doctrines and the poset of fragments of geometric logic as defined in the framework of Kan injectivity in the 2-category of topoi, recently developed by the first and third authors. Concretely, the connection is realised by a general construction of syntactic categories and classifying topoi which encompasses all well-known examples in categorical logic. This yields a mirror symmetry, between the algebraic world of doctrines and the geometric world of topoi: to provide evidence of this phenomenon, we prove a few symmetric results relating doctrines and logics by means of syntactic categories and classifying topoi. We conclude with a discussion on the theme of conceptual completeness.

Day Convolution and Homotopy Groups on the Pointed Homotopy Category of CW Complexes

Abhiram Srinivas Yedathore · 2610.29239
math.ATmath.CT
Abstract

We freely enrich the pointed homotopy category of CW complexes over abelian groups and equip the resulting functor category with the enriched Day convolution induced by the smash product. After identifying the convolution unit and recording the coherence maps, we compute the convolution of the homotopy-group functors. For integers $n,m\geq 2$ we construct a natural isomorphism $\pi_n\star\pi_m\cong\pi_{n+m}$ induced by the external smash product; equivalently, the external smash product is the universal natural pairing of homotopy groups. Under this identification, the symmetry interchanging the two factors acts on $\pi_{n+m}$ by multiplication by $(-1)^{nm}$.

Discrete homotopy of graphs and $k$-parabolic subspace arrangements

So Yamagata · 2610.29690
math.ATmath.CO
Abstract

Discrete homotopy theory is a combinatorial homotopy theory defined for discrete objects. Barcelo--Severs--White conjectured that the homotopy groups of complements of $k$-parabolic subspace arrangements are isomorphic to the discrete homotopy groups of graphs associated with the Coxeter complexes. We show that this correspondence fails in general by giving a counterexample in degree two for the $4$-parabolic subspace arrangement of type $A_5$.

Nielsen coincidence theory for $(n,m)$-valued pairs

Karel Dekimpe, Lore De Weerdt · 2610.29274
math.AT
Abstract

We define a Nielsen coincidence number for pairs of maps $(f,g)$ where $f$ is $n$-valued and $g$ is $m$-valued. The classical definition of the Nielsen number does not work in this setting, but we propose a theory where coincidence classes are allowed to overlap. We invoke a notion of multiplicity of a coincidence point, which indicates to how many classes it can belong, and we show that our Nielsen number is a homotopy-invariant lower bound for the number of coincidence points with multiplicity. We show a computational result in terms of single-valued lift factors, and using this, a Wecken property for self-maps of the circle.

Small covers and the Four Color Theorem

Suyoung Choi, Ringi Kim · 2610.29131
math.COmath.ATcross
Abstract

We give a combinatorial proof that every simple convex $3$-polytope admits a small cover without using the Four Color Theorem. We also prove that the assertion that every simple plane triangulation admits a mod~$2$ characteristic function with at most five values is equivalent to the Four Color Theorem.

Spanning bipartite quadrangulations in even triangulations of real projective spaces

Kengo Enami · 2610.29737
math.COmath.ATcross
Abstract

We study spanning quadrangulations of even triangulations of real projective spaces, using the higher-dimensional notion of quadrangulation introduced by Kaiser and Stehl\'{\i}k. We prove that every even triangulation of $\RP^d$, where $d\ge 2$, admits a spanning bipartite quadrangulation. Moreover, such a triangulation admits a spanning non-bipartite quadrangulation if and only if it is not balanced; if it is balanced, every spanning quadrangulation is bipartite. Our proofs use coloring monodromy: its orbits yield bipartite quadrangulations, while a covering-space construction gives non-bipartite ones. %In dimension three, a bipartite quadrangulation can always be chosen to induce $K_{2,2}$ on each tetrahedron. More generally, we establish a sufficient condition for the existence of spanning bipartite quadrangulations on closed manifolds in terms of the monodromy action, and derive a consequence involving the fundamental group.
