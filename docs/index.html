An elementary proof of rational injectivity in equivariant KK-theory

Denghui Luo, Geng Tian · 2610.31174
title: equivariantmath.KTmath.FAmath.OA
Abstract

Antonini, Azzali and Skandalis proved that, for every countable discrete group $\Gamma$, the canonical map $E\Gamma\to\underline{E}\Gamma$ induces a split injection in equivariant $KK$-theory with real coefficients. In this paper, we give a more elementary proof of the corresponding rational injectivity result in ordinary equivariant $KK$-theory without using $\mathrm{II}_1$ factors.

A K\"unneth theorem for the cyclic homology of A-infinity algebras

Henry Azubuike · 2610.30164
abstract: infinity categormath.KTmath.QAmath.SG
Abstract

The cyclic homology of a $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$-graded, smooth and proper $A_\infty$ category satisfying the Hodge-to-de-Rham degeneration property carries the structure of a polarized semi-infinite Hodge structure or the so-called EP-structure. Given two $A_\infty$ algebras $A$ and $B$ with the above conditions, we construct a K\"unneth map from the tensor product of their cyclic homologies to the cyclic homology of the $A_\infty$ tensor product $A\otimes_\infty B$ and show that it respects the EP-structures. As an application, we show that if $A$ and $B$ are equipped with weak Calabi-Yau structures, then $A\otimes_\infty B$ also inherits a weak Calabi-Yau structure. Also, we show that the K\"unneth quasi-isomorphism respects good splittings of the Hodge filtration on $A$ and $B$ compatible with the weak Calabi-Yau structure. Our explicit calculations rely on the combinatorial (tree) description of the tensor product of $A_\infty$ algebras developed in \cite{amorim2016tensor}.

Geometric chains of orbifolds with corners and singular homology

Hao Yu · 2610.28446
abstract: prismmath.GTmath.ATcross
Abstract

We study a geometric homology theory (GHT for short). Its chains are generated by smooth maps from compact oriented orbifolds with corners to a smooth space, and two kinds of chains are set to zero: those admitting an orientation reversing automorphism, and degenerate ones, which, together with their boundaries, factor through smooth maps to orbifolds of lower dimension. Bisecting the source of a geometric chain along a hypersurface transverse to its corners changes the chain only by the boundary of an explicit prism and by prisms over its faces; cutting a geometric cycle along a regular level set of a smooth function on the target does not change its homology class. Geometric chains also behave well under products and pullbacks along fibre bundles, which overcomes some of the combinatorial rigidity of singular chains. We prove that geometric homology is naturally isomorphic to singular homology with rational coefficients for every smooth space admitting a good cover, such as smooth manifolds, orbifolds and locally finite polyhedra, and also for orbifolds with corners. The proof uses cutting operators along level sets of functions on the target, realized by explicit prisms, instead of triangulations. For continuous maps into arbitrary spaces we conjecture the same statement, and prove it for Euclidean neighbourhood retracts in degrees at most one, with surjectivity in degree two. Without the relation killing degenerate chains the comparison fails for orbifold chains: the weighted projective plane $\mathbb{P}(1,1,p)$, $p\ge5$ prime, mapped to a point is a cycle that does not bound. Applications on Morse theory and the BV structure on geometric chains of moduli spaces of bordered Riemann surfaces, following Costello and Harrelson--Voronov--Z\'u\~niga, are given.

Isogenies of Oriented Elliptic Curves

Doron Grossman-Naples · 2610.30376
abstract: spectral algebraic geometrymath.ATmath.AG
Abstract

We construct and describe the moduli stack $\mathrm{Isog}$ of isogenies between oriented elliptic curves. We show that $\mathrm{Isog}$ is a spectral Deligne-Mumford stack and a relative spectral algebraic space over $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{ell}}^{\mathrm{or}}$, and give a fiber product decomposition of $\mathrm{Isog}$ corresponding to a connected-\'etale factorization on oriented elliptic curves. In the process of proving these results, we construct a theory of duality and quotients for spectral abelian varieties, prove a higher-algebraic version of Nakayama's lemma, and lift the rigidity theorem for morphisms of elliptic curves to the setting of spectral algebraic geometry.

Localizing Restriction Categories

Robin Cockett · 2610.30553
abstract: topoimath.CT
Abstract

The localizing/separating factorization, which is the analogue, for restriction categories, to the surjective/embedding factorization for topoi, is developed at both the mere restriction category and join restriction category level. The factorization system at both the ordinary and join level is approached using the notion of a density, which is a stable and universal suborder of the restriction order. The main technical results of the paper construct the join localization and establish its basic properties. The paper gives several examples of localizations. In particular, examples of localization on commutative rigs are provided which give a way to construct categories of schemes. The Zariski spectrum and the Pierce spectrum are, in particular, developed.

Bridging persistence of discrete and continuous 1D maps: Total variation, permutation equivalence, and diagonal affine transformations

Matthew Blake, Firas A. Khasawneh · 2610.31391
math.ATcs.CG
Abstract

Fundamental to the field of topological signal processing (TSP) is the \textit{persistence diagram} (PD), or barcode, of a signal, which gives noise-resistant measures of topologically significant features as distinct from noise. Existing literature proves that the total persistence computed from these diagrams is equal to the total variation, for certain continuous signals on compact domains. We provide a closed formula for this shared quantity, and generalize to all continuous and discrete functions on bounded domains, and to certain functions on non-compact 1D manifolds. Extending the identification of continuous and discrete persistence, we investigate the similarities of functions with the same PDs, and prove that, for any $n$, the persistence diagram can be calculated as a function of the sequence of critical values; we compute these formulas for $n \leq 6$. Finally, we examine which function transformations cause predictable changes in the PD of the transformed function, characterizing affine and linear transformations.

Landfall Geometry

Frances Kirwan, Vidit Nanda · 2610.30993
math.ATmath.DS
Abstract

We study the negative gradient flow of analytic maps $f:X\to\R$ defined on compact analytic Riemannian manifolds without imposing any nondegeneracy or transversality assumptions. We construct tight isolating blocks around the connected components of the critical set $\Crit(f)$ and record only bounded-time contacts made after an orbit exits one block and before it makes landfall within the interior of another. These contacts assemble into a strict topological category $\cL$ -- called the landfall category -- whose classifying space $B\cL$ is shown to be simple homotopy equivalent to $X$ naturally over all down-sets of the flow partial order. The filtered homotopy type is independent of the isolating blocks. Passing to a finite subanalytic triangulation yields a flow-induced acyclic partial matching, and hence, an explicit finite chain complex generated by the non-exit simplices of the blocks which computes the homology of $X$. The finite model also computes the Morse--Conley spectral sequence over arbitrary coefficients; over a field, homological perturbation further produces a connection matrix whose entries are finite sums over descending contact itineraries.

Mathematical AI Predicts 40% and 57% Influenza Vaccine Effectiveness Against H1N1 and H3N2, Respectively, for the 2026--2027 U.S. Season

Xiang Xiang Wang, Guo-Wei Wei · 2610.31301
math.AT
Abstract

Background: Clinical influenza vaccine effectiveness (VE) has often been low over the past two decades, highlighting the need for preseason VE forecasting. Timely forecasts could help health authorities plan prevention strategies and allocate healthcare resources. However, preseason forecasting of clinical influenza VE remains relatively uncommon. Methods: We obtained hemagglutinin (HA) sequences from GISAID for U.S. specimens collected in August--September before each influenza season from 2010--2011 through 2024--2025. After quality screening, we compared each virus with the corresponding vaccine target strain for the same season and subtype or lineage using two mathematical distances based on amino acid patterns in the HA protein. We calculated mean distances for each season and subtype or lineage and paired them with published clinical VE estimates, yielding 26 observations across 13 seasons. Using a mathematical AI approach, we fitted linear regression models of VE against each distance and evaluated their predictive performance by forecasting later seasons using data from earlier seasons, with the historical mean VE for each subtype or lineage as a reference. We then applied the same approach to 87 HA sequences collected in August--September 2026 to forecast VE for the 2026--2027 season. Results: We generated component-specific forecasts of influenza VE for the upcoming 2026--2027 U.S. season. Both mathematical distances were negatively associated with historical VE, indicating that greater differences between circulating viruses and vaccine strains were associated with lower VE. The topological model forecast VE of 40.3%, 56.9%, and 47.1% against A(H1N1)pdm09, A(H3N2), and B/Victoria, respectively, whereas the geometric model forecast corresponding VE values of 34.0%, 56.8%, and 51.1%.

The center is the formal internal automorphism group

Sonja Farr · 2610.31411
math.ATmath.AG
Abstract

We prove that the deformation functor associated to an algebra over a sufficiently nice Koszul operad can be lifted to an operadic formal moduli problem over the Koszul dual operad. We further show that this operadic formal moduli problem corresponds to the derived center of the algebra under the operadic Lurie-Pridham correspondence. As an application, we prove that the classical Gerstenhaber algebra structure on polyvector fields of a commutative algebra agrees with the derived center of the algebra as an abelian Poisson algebra.

Triangulation of orbifolds with corners

Hao Yu · 2610.31001
math.GTmath.ATcross
Abstract

We prove that every compact orbifold with corners admits a triangulation in which the image of every codimension one face is a subcomplex. Faces need not be embedded, and the local groups may permute boundary components. The proof turns the boundary hyperplanes of linear charts into mirrors by means of the squaring map, which, unlike the folding $y\mapsto|y|$, is compatible with smooth changes of charts, and then applies the triangulation theorem for orbit spaces of proper Lie groupoids.

Vanishing of Higher Magnitude Homology for Finite Prefix Models

Ryoji Hirayama · 2610.00445
math.CTmath.ATHexagonAI-generated (no human author claimed)
Abstract

Bradley and Vigneaux define a generalized distance on a finite prefix poset using next-token probabilities. They conjecture that its higher magnitude homology vanishes. Motivated by their expression of magnitude as a weighted sum of homological Euler characteristics, we verify this conjecture for their explicit complex, in which both endpoint faces are zero. For each finite length, the homology groups in degrees two and above are zero. The degree-one homology group is free abelian, with one generator for each positive-probability edge of that length. The proof constructs a contracting homotopy for each component whose endpoints have an interior prefix. This homotopy inserts the least interior prefix. Edge probabilities zero and one are both allowed.
