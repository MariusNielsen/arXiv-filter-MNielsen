The generalized Segal conjecture and the coalgebraic Frobenius

Thomas Nikolaus · 2611.33372
author: Thomas Nikolausabstract: algebraic k-theormath.ATmath.KT
Abstract

We prove the generalized Segal conjecture, extending work of Bergsaker and Rognes and confirming a conjecture by the author: for every finite group $G$ and every bounded-below spectrum $X$, the Hill--Hopkins--Ravenel norm $N_e^G X$ becomes Borel-complete after completion at the augmentation ideal of the Burnside ring. Equivalently, for every non-trivial finite $p$-group $G$, the proper Tate diagonal $X\to(X^{\otimes G})^{\varphi G}$ is a $p$-completion. The proof reduces to the case $X=\Fp$, established by Bergsaker and Rognes, and removes the finiteness hypotheses from their theorem by abstract manipulations. As an application of this result, we refine the coalgebraic Frobenius to an oriented circle action (i.e. an action of the monoidal category $B\mathbb N$) on the category of bounded-below, $p$-complete $\mathbb{E}_\infty$-coalgebras, confirming another conjecture by the author. The construction is based on Yuan's approach to Frobenius actions via partial algebraic $K$-theory.

Equivariant $K$-homology of affine Grassmannians of orthogonal groups

Koushik Brahma, Takeshi Ikeda, Mark Shimozono · 2611.34174
title: equivariantmath.AGmath.COmath.KTmath.RT
Abstract

We construct explicit symmetric-function realizations of the torus-equivariant $K$-homology of affine Grassmannians of types B and D. More precisely, we realize the extended $K$-theoretic Peterson algebras as subalgebras of a completed ring of equivariant symmetric functions and identify their Schubert classes in terms of symmetric functions. We introduce two new families of equivariant symmetric functions for each of types B and D and prove that these functions represent the classes of the structure sheaves of Schubert varieties and the ideal sheaves of their boundaries, respectively. We establish several fundamental properties of these functions, including translation factorization formulas and explicit expressions for the classes indexed by small affine Grassmannian elements. In type D, we further describe the action of the affine Dynkin diagram automorphism interchanging the two spin nodes and show how it intertwines the Schubert bases with a nontrivial transformation of the equivariant parameters.

Higher rewriting for strong linearly distributive coherence

Max Demirdilek, Christian Reiher, Christoph Schweigert · 2611.34596
abstract: higher categormath.CTcs.LOmath.QA
Abstract

Rewriting is a computational method to solve word problems: deciding whether two compound expressions, or words, are equivalent with respect to a set of identities. Higher rewriting generalizes rewriting to higher-dimensional words, or cells, in higher categories. Following Guiraud and Malbos, we use higher rewriting to generalize Mac Lane's coherence theorem. Our result concerns strong linearly distributive categories: categories equipped with two unital tensor products related by invertible distributors. We show that in a strong linearly distributive category every formal diagram commutes.

$E_d$-Enhancements of $L_\infty$-Algebras Associated to Secondary Polytopes

Longfei Li · 2611.34431
math.AT
Abstract

We study $E_d$-enhancements of the geometric $L_\infty$-algebras associated to secondary polytopes of finite configurations in $\mathbb R^d$, $d\geq2$. We construct an action of the semialgebraic chains of the Fulton--MacPherson operad on the shifted algebra $B_A=\mathfrak g_A[1-d]$ when $|A|\leq3d$. The action recovers the prescribed higher Lie operations on fundamental chains. The construction is obtained from a general extension theorem for weighted shifted $L_\infty$-algebras, using reflection to control relative cohomology obstructions. For $|A|\leq3d-1$, the resulting reflection-compatible integration actions are homotopic relative to their shifted Lie structure. We also give an abstract enhancement without a cardinality bound, direct formulas for circuits, and explicit operations for convex pentagons and hexagons.

Deleted square of a cone over a graph

Andrzej T. Karolak · 2611.33558
math.AT
Abstract

The deleted square of a topological space is a subspace of the Cartesian product of this space with itself, obtained by removing the diagonal. Conjecturally, the deleted square of a cone over a graph has the homotopy type of a wedge sum of spheres of dimensions at most 3. We propose a geometric construction that is a key motivation for the conjecture and provides a major step towards its proof, while yielding direct results on deleted squares of certain families of graphs.

Explicit covers and rigid obstructions for simplicial complexity: from spheres to surfaces

M. Perez, J. Flores · 2611.33804
math.ATcs.ROmath.CO
Abstract

The strict simplicial complexity $SC_{\rm strict}(K)$, Gonz\'alez's simplicial complexity at subdivision level zero, is one less than the least number of contiguity domains covering the ordered product $K\times K$. We bound it from both sides. For upper bounds, every product of two vertex stars is a domain, which gives $SC_{\rm strict}(K)\le\rho(K)-1$ for an exactly computable set-cover number $\rho(K)$; the bound is sharp on every even-dimensional simplex boundary. A GPU annealer that evolves whole systems of contiguity chains finds three-domain covers of $\partial\Delta^3,\dots,\partial\Delta^7$ and of the cube boundary. Seeded by star covers, it descends from $24$ to $5$ domains on the icosahedron boundary, and an exact SAT completion of its stalled state gives $SC_{\rm strict}\le3$ there. For lower bounds, a subcomplex of $K\times K$ is rigid if its two projections are joined by no contiguity chain of any length; $k$ domains can cover $K\times K$ only if $k$ classes of facets avoid every rigid subcomplex, a finite SAT problem. Rigid subcomplexes arise from hollow triangles over essential empty triangles and, on complexes without dominated vertices, from the graph of every order-preserving simplicial self-map other than the identity. The resultingchain-length-free bounds give $SC_{\rm strict}(\partial\Delta^{n+1})=2>1=TC(S^n)$ for $n=3,5,7,9$, $SC_{\rm strict}=2>1$ for the five-vertex M\"obius band, $SC_{\rm strict}\ge3>2$ for the seven-vertex torus, and $4\le SC_{\rm strict}\le5$ and $4\le SC_{\rm strict}\le6$ for two triangulations of $\mathbb{RP}^2$. They also sort the annealer's stalls: on these two triangulations of $\mathbb{RP}^2$, the Klein bottle and $\partial\Delta^4$, almost every uncovered facet closes a rigid subcomplex in every domain, while the icosahedron stall was a search failure.

Face posets, local weight matroids and Betti numbers of torus actions

David Mikheev · 2611.01086
math.ATmath.AGmath.COmath.RTHexagonAI-generated (no human author claimed)
Abstract

Let a compact torus act on a closed manifold $X$ with isolated fixed points. The face poset $S(X)$ of the action is covered by the lattices of flats of the linear matroids of tangent weights at the fixed points. We study how much of the cohomology of $X$ these combinatorial data see. Local equivariant cohomology. The group $H^*_T(X, X\setminus pt)$, embedded in $H^*_T(pt)$ by restriction, is the principal ideal generated by the product of the tangent weights. It determines the weights up to scalars, with multiplicities, and hence the local lattice $S(X)_{\geq pt}$. Local matroids do not suffice. Two connected regular semisimple Hessenberg varieties in $\mathrm{Fl}(\mathbb{C}^5)$, with torus actions of complexity one, have isomorphic weight matroids at all fixed points, and hence the same Chow rings, Orlik–Solomon algebras and Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials there, but different Betti numbers. The face poset determines the Betti numbers of every regular semisimple Hessenberg variety of type A, full or partial, and of the isospectral manifolds of Ayzenberg and Buchstaber. The reason is rigidity: the cluster-permutohedron $\mathrm{Cl}_\Gamma$ determines the graph $\Gamma$, by an elementary argument that recovers the vertices of $\Gamma$ from stars of edges. Simply-laced types. The same holds for regular semisimple Hessenberg varieties of types A, D and E: the face poset determines the Weyl group together with the lines spanned by the roots of the Hessenberg ideal, up to isometry. A formula. The Poincaré polynomial is a sum over the face poset, $P_X(q)=\sum_{\sigma\in S(X)}(q-1)^{\mathrm{rk}\,\sigma}e_\sigma(q)$, in which $e_\sigma$ is a sum of Tutte polynomials of the matroids of weights that are negative on a generic circle. For toric manifolds this is the $h$-vector formula.

Graded Monoidal Categories and Internalization

Bryce Goldman · 2611.33663
math.CTmath.AT
Abstract

In stable homotopy theory, there are a variety of ways to construct a point-set level smash product of spectra, each with its own advantages and limitations. These constructions often involve internalizing some graded external product. In this paper, we aim to formalize this process through a general categorical framework, providing a common language for these constructions. We propose a general theory for internalizing a graded monoidal product, investigate its 2-categorical properties, and use this framework to recover both Day convolution and the smash product of $S$-modules.

The pentagonal tensor and the fourth obstruction to formality

Chaoshun Yang, Xiugui Liu · 2611.34315
math.AT
Abstract

Let \(A\) be a connected graded commutative differential graded algebra over \(\mathbb Q\) whose cohomology is a finite-dimensional Poincar\'e duality algebra of formal dimension \(N\ge2\). Assuming that its Bianchi-Massey tensor vanishes, we prove that the normalized pentagonal tensor of Nagy and Nordstr\"om, in [preprint (2021), arXiv:2105.13660], vanishes if and only if the fourth Harrison obstruction vanishes after eliminating the third operation. This gives a fourth-order counterpart to the third-order comparison of Fiorenza and L\^e, in [Ann. Sc. Norm. Super. Pisa Cl. Sci. (5), published online 7 March 2025], and answers the vanishing question in L\^e's proposed comparison, in [preprint (2026), arXiv:2603.01219], under the hypothesis that the Bianchi-Massey tensor vanishes. The result applies to closed, connected, oriented manifolds with no upper dimension bound. We also extend Zhou's sphere-bundle formality criterion in [Algebr. Geom. Topol. 25 (2025), pp. 2297--2315] to two-stage oriented sphere bundles with positive odd fibre dimensions over a closed, connected, oriented, rationally formal base, provided that the second Euler class is pulled back from the original base. Their total spaces are rationally formal precisely when the Bianchi-Massey and normalized pentagonal tensors vanish. Our method develops the model-based strategy in Zhou's proof within a commutative bar-cobar framework, providing a starting point for constructing higher tensors and comparing them with higher-order formality obstructions.
