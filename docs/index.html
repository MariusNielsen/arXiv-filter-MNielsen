The $RO(C_2)$-graded Hurewicz image of Atiyah's Real K-theory

Sihao Ma, Xiaolin Danny Shi, Zhouli Xu, Albert Jinghui Yang, Shangjie Zhang · 2610.19606
author: Zhouli Xuabstract: stable stemsmath.AT
Abstract

We determine the $RO(C_2)$-graded $k\mathbb R$-Hurewicz image, where $k \mathbb R$ denotes the connective Atiyah's Real $K$-theory spectrum. As a consequence, we obtain four infinite families in the $RO(C_2)$-graded equivariant stable stems that are $C_2$-equivariant refinements of the classical $\mu$-families. Using a $C_2$-equivariant $v_{1,0}$-self map, we explicitly construct an equivariant Adams periodicity operator $P_{C_2}$ that captures these $v_1$-periodicity phenomena in the $C_2$-stable stems.

Dylan Pentland · 2610.20466
title: prismaticmath.AGmath.NT
Abstract

We study the category of constructible $p$-Kummer-\'{e}tale sheaves on the log adic generic fiber $(\mathrm{X},\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}})_\eta$ for the stratification induced by a horizontal normal crossings divisor $\mathrm{D}$, showing that it is antiequivalent to a category of finitely generated unit Laurent crystals on the log prismatic site. The classical homotopy category is cartesian closed

Zhen Lin Low · 2610.20428
abstract: (infinity, 1)math.ATmath.CT
Abstract

The category of all topological spaces and homotopy classes of maps is cartesian closed. In fact, the $(\infty, 1)$-category of all topological spaces and continuous maps, localised at homotopy equivalences, is cartesian closed. We model the exponential object using a topological space whose points are continuous maps equipped with putative neighbourhood filters. In general, this topological space will not even be $T_1$-separated, but the evaluation map induces a natural weak homotopy equivalence witnessing the universal property.

$\mathbb{Z}_k$-smooth approximation of mod $k$ integral cycles

Gianmarco Caldini, Anna Skorobogatova · 2610.20499
math.DGmath.ATcross
Abstract

We prove that every mod $k$ integral cycle $T$ of sufficiently high codimension in an oriented Riemannian manifold $\mathcal{M}$ can be approximated in flat norm by a cycle which is a $\mathbb{Z}_k$-smooth submanifold $\Sigma$ of nearly the same area, up to a singular set of codimension 5. If in addition $T$ belongs to a mod $k$ homology class admitting an embedded $\mathbb{Z}_k$-smooth manifold representative, then $\Sigma$ can be taken fully regular, up to the codimension one stratum of classical singularities. This extends the smooth approximation theory to cycles with coefficients in $\mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z}$ in stable regimes. Moreover, we provide examples showing the impossibility of locally approximating minimizing cycles by smooth or $\mathbb{Z}_k$-smooth submanifolds, even in homology classes with smooth or $\mathbb{Z}_k$-smooth representatives.

Anomalies, higher symmetries, and homotopy theory I: crossed precosheaves and a nonabelian Cech construction

Anton Kapustin, Shixiong Xu · 2610.19004
math-phcond-mat.str-elhep-thmath.ATcross
Abstract

Symmetries of a quantum field theory or a quantum lattice model are expected to be described not by a group but by a homotopy type, whose homotopy groups are the higher symmetry groups and whose Postnikov invariants encode 't Hooft anomalies. We propose a homotopy-theoretic definition of 't Hooft anomalies based on the structure of localized symmetries in the operator-algebraic approach. The input is a crossed precosheaf: a collection of groups attached to regions of space, equipped with pairings recording that the commutator of two transformations localized in two regions is localized in their intersection. To a crossed precosheaf and a finite family of regions we attach a pointed connected homotopy type, the Cech-Loday classifying space. As the family of regions ranges over finite covers of space, the classifying spaces form a pro-object in the homotopy category. There is a canonical map from the ordinary classifying space of the symmetry group to this pro-type. Composing this map with a group action gives an anomaly whose nontriviality obstructs gauging. In favorable cases, the Cech-Loday space is essentially independent of the cover. In such cases, the homotopy fiber of the canonical map is a candidate for the classifying space of the higher group of symmetries. We give examples of crossed precosheaves arising from gauge transformations in continuum field theory and from quantum cellular automata and finite-depth circuits. For quantum spin systems in one and two dimensions, our construction recovers the Else-Nayak index and its generalizations to two dimensions.

Discrete Morse Theory for Computing Filtered Cosheaf Homology

Neria Baris · 2610.20376
math.AT
Abstract

This paper addresses the computation of persistent homology for filtrations of cellular cosheaves over a fixed cell complex, where the algebraic data vary along the filtration while the underlying topological structure remains unchanged. We extend methods from discrete Morse theory to this setting, establishing conditions under which chain complexes can be reduced while preserving the associated persistence module. Building on these results, we introduce "FilteredScythe", an algorithm that identifies Morse matchings compatible with all stages of the filtration and performs reductions using Schur complements, based on the exist "Scythe" algorithm. We prove the correctness of these reductions and establish bounds on the algorithm's time and space complexity.

This is my written Laudatio for Graeme Segal on receiving the Chern Medal at the 2026 International Congress of Mathematicians. I make some general remarks about Segal's qualities as a mathematician. Real Tree Vineyards have Vines

Michael Hull, Thomas Payne, Thomas Weighill · 2610.20046
math.AT
Abstract

We show that every vineyard can be decomposed into vines, that is, every path in the space of persistence diagrams $\mathcal{D}$ can be decomposed into a sequence of paths in $\mathbb R^2$. Moreover, we characterize which spaces $X$ have the property that every continuous map $X\to \mathcal{D}$ has such a decomposition. Our results hold not just for $\mathcal{D}$, but also for many other spaces of unordered tuples.
