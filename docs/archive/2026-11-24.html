The Balmer spectrum of integral permutation modules

Umesh V Dubey, Juan Omar G\'omez · 2507.05892
author: Juan Omar Gómeztitle: balmer spectrmath.RTmath.AGmath.CTreplace
Abstract

We study the Balmer spectrum of the small derived category of permutation modules of a finite group over a commutative Noetherian ring. In this generality, we obtain a set-theoretic description of the spectrum. Under certain mild additional assumptions on the base ring, we describe its topology by reducing the problem first to the modular locus and then to the case of elementary abelian groups. Furthermore, we introduce a twisted cohomology ring in this context, which allows us to realize the spectrum, in the case of elementary abelian $p$-groups, as a Dirac scheme. This extends recent work of Balmer and Gallauer from the setting of ground fields to the more general setting of commutative Noetherian rings.

Divisibility of invariant derivatives on formal groups of every height, on a question of Katz

Floris Alderliefste · 2611.00938
abstract: lubin-tatemath.NTmath.AGHexagonAI-generated (no human author claimed)
Abstract

In 1977 Katz asked for an explicit lower bound on the $p$-adic valuation of $(D^nf)(0)$, where $D$ is the normalized invariant derivation of a one-parameter formal group of finite height $h$ over a ring of $p$-adic integers and $f$ is a power series with integral coefficients. He answered the question at height two, for functions whose translates by the points of order $p$ sum to zero, with the bound $\lfloor np/(p^2-1)\rfloor$. For Lubin--Tate groups over unramified bases, results of Bannai and Kobayashi give the bound $c_hn-(h-1)$, where $c_h=\frac1{p-1}-\frac1{p^h-1}$. We prove this bound for every one-dimensional formal group whose multiplication by $p$ is divisible by $p$ below degree $p^h$, over any $p$-torsion-free $\mathbb Z_{(p)}$-algebra and for every integral $f$, together with the bound $\sum_{h\nmid j}\lfloor n/p^j\rfloor$. The slope $c_h$, the constant $h-1$, and the second bound at the powers of $p$ cannot be improved. At height two it follows that Katz's bound holds for every integral function except at the orders divisible by $p^2-1$, where it can fail by one, and it does fail at the first of them. Over a ramified base the hypothesis cannot be weakened to the height of the special fibre, while an action of the Teichmüller roots of unity restores it. For the Cartier dual of the Honda formal group of height $h$ we construct an integral model whose $h-1$ coordinate directions have the sharp slopes $p^i/(p^h-1)$, which add up to $c_h$, each with the optimal constant $1$. The height-two case is the local exponent in the second step of the timeline in Alpöge's note on the Riemann hypothesis, where the bound is stated with a logarithmic loss; for integral functions on the formal group the loss is the constant $1$.
