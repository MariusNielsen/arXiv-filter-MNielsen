Abstract

We exhibit the $p$-complete $\mathbb{C}$-motivic Eilenberg-Mac Lane spectra $\mathrm{M}\mathbb{F}_p$ and $(\mathrm{M}\mathbb{Z})_p^{\wedge}$ as $\mathbb{E_3}$-$\mathrm{MGL}_p^{\wedge}$-motivic Thom spectra over the motivic $\mathbb{G}_m$-loop spaces $\Omega_{\mathbb{G}_m}\mathrm{BGL}$ and $\Omega_{\mathbb{G}_m}\mathrm{BSL}$, respectively. An explicit Dennis trace and its compatibility with Koszul duality

Maozhou Huang · 2610.25485
title: koszul dualabstract: comoduleabstract: algebraic k-theormath.KT
Abstract

In this paper, we construct explicit Dennis trace maps from algebraic $K$-theory to Hochschild homology for dg module and curved dg comodule categories, and prove their compatibility with derived Koszul duality. For this, we construct a natural chain map $\tau$ from the chains on the newly defined thickened nerve to the total complex of the dg cyclic nerve, with explicit formulas incorporating morphisms and coherent higher homotopies, which generalizes McCarthy's approach. This $\tau$ construction works both for modules over dg algebras and for cdg comodules over counital conilpotent curved dg coalgebras. Combining the $\tau$ with the dg-enriched Waldhausen $S_\bullet$-construction and a Hochschild delooping theorem yields the Dennis trace maps. For thick subcategories corresponding under derived Koszul duality, we prove that the induced $K$-theory equivalences and Hochschild homology isomorphisms fit into natural commutative diagrams with these trace maps.

Cospan contexts and higher categories

Lorenzo Riva · 2610.24473
title: higher categormath.CTmath.AT
Abstract

Motivated by the desire to turn the cobordism hypothesis (a statement about the universal property of some $n$-category of bordisms) into a $1$-categorical statement, we prove that all $n$-categories can be modeled by \emph{$n$-cospan contexts}: these are $1$-categories equipped with a collection of iterated cospans that satisfy a composition property in terms of pushouts. In particular, we construct an adjunction from $\mathsf{Cat}_n$ to the category of $n$-cospan contexts which simultaneously generalizes the $n$-fold span construction of Haugseng and the twisted arrow category construction (when $n = 1$).

On simplicial maps with non-zero Hopf invariant

Sergei Vad. Fomin · 2610.25562
title: hopf invariantmath.ATmath.CO
Abstract

In general, it is interesting to investigate the complexity of simplicial maps under various topological constraints. In this paper, we consider maps between (triangulated oriented rational homology) manifolds with non-zero Hopf invariant and establish bounds on the simplicial complexity of the fiber of a regular point. The lower bound, which is cubic in the dimension, is proved via a refinement of a theorem of S. Eilenberg. The upper bound is notable in that it is uniform with respect to the Hopf invariant.

On the notion of $\mathbb{A}^1$-coverings

Marco Giustetto, Thor Wittich · 2610.26345
abstract: infinity-topomath.ATmath.AG
Abstract

We introduce the notion of $\mathbb{A}^1$-$n$-coverings and begin the development of their theory over an arbitrary base scheme $S$, in analogy to that of higher coverings in an $\infty$-topos. We show that for $n=0$, our notion recovers Morel's $\mathbb{A}^1$-coverings between simplicial sheaves. Under suitable assumptions, we then provide a Galois-theoretic classification of $\mathbb{A}^1$-$n$-coverings. Finally, we answer a question of Morel concerning a geometric criterion for $\mathbb{A}^1$-coverings in the negative.

A characterization of finite modular 2-groups via magnitude homology

Luigi Caputi, Francesco G. Russo · 2610.25030
math.GRmath.ATmath.COcross
Abstract

Finite quasihamiltonian groups - that is, finite groups in which every pair of subgroups commutes - admit several characterizations. In this work, after introducing the notion of sectional contractibility graph, we provide a new characterization in terms of (a suitable subgroup of) magnitude homology. Our approach especially focuses on the subgroup lattices of finite groups and on the related notion of modularity. We present a detailed study for finite 2-groups, and, in particular, in the case of extraspecial 2-groups and Pauli groups. These groups, of interest because of their diverse applications in both mathematics and physics, already describe the wealth of information that one can obtain in restricting the study to the case of 2-groups.

A New Proof of Wei's Theorem

Zhiqaing Wei, Mengyan Xing, Xingya Zhao · 2610.25838
math.AT
Abstract

In the paper \emph{Structure and realizability for rational maps}, Wei proved the following. Let \(k\) nontrivial partitions of an integer \(d\ge 3\) be given, satisfying the Riemann--Hurwitz formula for the case of maps between two spheres. If \(k>\ell+1\), where \[ \ell=\min_i|\pi_i| \] is the shortest partition length, then there exists a rational map whose branch datum is given by these partitions. In this paper we give a new proof of Wei's theorem on realizing a tuple of partitions as the ramification data of a rational function. The proof is inductive on the shortest partition length. Two auxiliary results are established: a two-cut partition theorem and a gluing theorem for marked spherical coverings. These reduce any counterexample with shortest length \(\ell\ge3\) to auxiliary data with either fewer factors or smaller shortest length, while preserving the total defect and the marked conditions. The base case \(\ell\le2\) is quoted from the literature.

Adelic Asphericity and One-Relator Pro-\(p\) Groups

Andrey Mikhovich · 2610.26288
math.ATmath.GR
Abstract

We give a direct Fox-calculus proof of the known rational asphericity theorem for every finite presentation complex with one nontrivial relator, including proper powers. The proof uses completed Fox derivatives and the absence of zero divisors in the completed algebra of a prounipotent group. Exact scalar extension to the finite adeles then yields adelic asphericity and a product decomposition of the higher homotopy groups of integral Bousfield--Kan completion. We also prove rational and pro-\(p\) analogues of Mannan's formula for finite two-complexes, with their natural topologies and fundamental group actions. The rational cohomological Whitehead criterion is proved directly. For a discrete one-relator group with relator not a proper power, we show that the tower \(L_2\nu_n(G)\) is Mittag--Leffler. Consequently \(\limone_nL_2\nu_n(G)=0\), and \(\pi_2(\Z_\infty BG)\) is naturally the inverse limit of the nilpotent Baer invariants. We identify this Baer limit canonically and topologically with the kernel of the integral augmentation-completed Fox operator and with the product of its local pro-\(p\) kernels. The comparison yields a torsion-free profinite homotopy module, vanishing inverse limits of the finite-stage transgression kernels, and explicit lifting obstructions. These results provide a homotopy-theoretic framework for the question of Serre in Gildenhuys's corrected formulation.

An Atiyah-Segal homomorphism for approximate representations of infinite groups, and almost multiplicative ucp maps

Rufus Willett · 2610.24252
math.KTmath.ATmath.GRmath.OA
Abstract

The Atiyah-Segal homomorphism relates the representation ring of a finite group to the $K$-theory ring of its classifying space. We develop a version of this for infinite groups, but based on a ring of approximate representations rather than representations. We then use known results on the Baum-Connes conjecture to show that our homomorphism is an isomorphism for many groups: this includes all torsion-free amenable groups, and all torsion-free fundamental groups of compact manifolds of dimension at most three. The main motivation is the desire to understand approximate representations using topological tools: as a sample application we give some examples of torsion phenomena in approximate representation theory. The methods of proof are $C^*$-algebraic: the key idea is to relate our Atiyah-Segal type homomorphism to Kasparov's coassembly map. The main step realizes (Hausdorffized) $KK$-theory as approximately multiplicative ucp maps modulo stable approximate unitary equivalence for appropriate $C^*$-algebras. This is in the spirit of the stable uniqueness theorems of Dadarlat-Eilers and Lin, and may be of purely $C^*$-algebraic interest.

Homotopy distributions for Vietoris-Rips complexes of random circle samples

Jaemin Park (Department of Mathematics, Research Institute of Natural Sciences, Sookmyung Women's University) · 2610.26366
math.PRmath.ATcross
Abstract

We determine the distribution of the homotopy type of the Vietoris-Rips complex of a fixed number of independent uniform points on a circle. An exact identity expresses each binomial moment of a periodic-orbit count as one probability for circular gaps, multiplied by a constant avoidance factor. We evaluate these probabilities by finite lattice-point counts and interpolation, obtaining explicit formulas for every sample size and every real scale. On an explicit rational partition, the probabilities are continuous piecewise polynomials of degree at most one less than the sample size. We also determine the homotopy distribution in the first transition window above each positive critical fraction $c_t=t/(2t+1)$. In this window the number of critical periodic orbits converges to a Poisson law with explicit intensity, yielding the limiting probabilities of the preceding odd sphere, the contractible type, and every wedge multiplicity of the critical even sphere.

Negative-Definite Plumbings and New Unknotting Numbers

Seong-Jin Lee · 2610.25430
math.GThep-thmath.ATcross
Abstract

We use Neumann's normal form to decide whether the double branched cover of a knot bounds a negative-definite plumbing tree in either orientation, starting from the recognised Seifert or graph-manifold structure of the cover. For many non-alternating knots with at most thirteen crossings whose unknotting numbers were unknown, this identifies the cases in which the Heegaard Floer homology of the cover can be computed from such a plumbing, and shows for most of the others that it cannot. We determine 112 new unknotting numbers. For 43 of them, the lower bound rests on Heegaard Floer homology computed from the plumbing; for the other 69, it comes from linking pairings and from a theorem of McCoy and Park on special alternating knots. Finally, we show that the knot 13n1587 is a further explicit counterexample to the Bernhard--Jablan conjecture, after 13n3370.

The minimum number of vertices of a triangulation of $\mathbb{RP}^5$

Xinrui Chang (Fudan University) · 2610.25171
math.COmath.ATmath.GTcross
Abstract

We give a computer-assisted proof that every finite simplicial triangulation of real projective five-space has at least twenty-four vertices. Together with the construction of Guyer, Steinerberger, and Yang, this determines the minimum. A cup-product argument produces an induced subcomplex on sixteen or seventeen vertices. Transfer to the spherical double cover then imposes a covering condition on its lifted faces. In the sixteen-vertex case, a direct capacity bound excludes twenty-two vertices. For twenty-three vertices, a cochain extension argument reduces every relevant completion to one of 115 critical signed complexes; integer weights and exact covering certificates exclude all of them. The argument requires no assumption that the triangulation is combinatorial.
