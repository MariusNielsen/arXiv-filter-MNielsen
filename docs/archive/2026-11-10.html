An introduction to discrete dynamics in condensed sets: orbits, periodic loci, and arithmetic examples

J. Rogelio P\'erez-Buend\'ia · 2611.10438
title: condensed setmath.DSmath.CTmath.NTcross
Abstract

We formulate discrete dynamics internally in the topos Cond of condensed sets. For an endomorphism $f\colon X\to X$, we construct orbit quotients, backward histories, preimage trees, and periodic loci as objects evaluated on profinite parameter spaces. The framework recovers global-section dynamics, fully faithfully embeds compact Hausdorff systems, characterizes the invertible extension, and is compatible with inverse limits. It organizes $p$-adic congruence towers and shows, for a Cantor-type non-Archimedean Julia set, that classical periodic points can be dense although the internal periodic locus is not isomorphic to the compact condensed Julia set.

FRT presentation of the $q$-Onsager comodule algebra and refined $q$-characters

Pascal Baseilhac, Azat M. Gainutdinov · 2611.12874
title: comodulemath.QAmath-phmath.MPmath.RTcross
Abstract

We introduce a concept of counital comodule algebra $(B,\delta_B,\epsilon_B)$ over a Hopf ${\mathbb K}$-algebra $H$, where $\delta_B: B\rightarrow B \otimes H$ and $\epsilon_B: B\rightarrow {\mathbb K}$ are coaction and counit homomorphisms, respectively. This structure gives a canonical (counital) comodule algebra map $\iota_B: B \rightarrow H$. In this paper, we study in details the case $B=O_q$, and $H$ is the quantum loop algebra ${\cal L}U_qsl_2$ for ${\mathbb K}={\mathbb C}$. Here, $O_q$ is the $q$-Onsager algebra with four presentations: (i) the $q$-Dolan-Grady type; (ii) the alternating type; (iii) the root vectors type; (iv) the Drinfeld type. For $(O_q,\delta_{O_q},\epsilon_{O_q})$, in fact a 3-parameter family of counital comodule algebras, we establish a Faddeev-Reshetikhin-Takhtajan (FRT) type presentation, which is written in terms of a K-operator satisfying a reflection algebra relation. The coaction $\delta_{O_q}$ (resp. $\iota_{O_q}$) sends the K-operator to a `dressed' K-operator (resp. K-matrix) using Ding-Frenkel type L-operators of ${\cal L}U_qsl_2$; the counit $\epsilon_{O_q}$ sends the K-operator to the most general (non-diagonal) solution of the reflection equation. The FRT presentation allows to compute the image via $\delta_{O_q}$ and $\iota_{O_q}$ of the generating functions of $O_q$ for each presentation (ii)-(iv). Moreover, the images of Lu-Wang's generating functions of $O_q$ in the so-called Drinfeld second realization ${}^{Dr}\!{\cal L}U_qsl_2$ are obtained, as well as their images via the (ordinary) coproduct of ${}^{Dr}\!{\cal L}U_qsl_2$, which enhance two theorems by Prze\'zdziecki. Finally, we apply our FRT presentation to the construction of new type $q$-characters for ${\cal L}U_qsl_2$, in imaginary root vectors of $O_q$, refining those of Frenkel-Reshetikhin, and we explain representation-theoretic meaning of such refining.

Multiplicativity of the Serre Spectral Sequence in Homotopy Type Theory

Arseniy Kryazhev · 2611.11828
title: spectral sequencemath.ATmath.CTmath.LO
Abstract

Working in homotopy type theory, we define relative homotopy groups and give three equivalent definitions of the product on these groups. Using this product, we establish the multiplicativity of the cohomological Serre spectral sequence. We show a connection between the homotopy product and the cup-product in cohomology, which leads to the classically familiar description of the starting page of the spectral sequence as a tensor product of graded rings. As applications, we compute cohomology rings of several classifying spaces and loop spaces of spheres.

Abstract

We make two case studies of $\mathrm{RO}(G)$-graded homotopy in connection with $p$-adic cohomology. Motivic Mirror Symmetry for Sink-recurrent Cluster Varieties

Haoyang Liu, Tianle Liu · 2611.12863
abstract: algebraic k-theormath.AG
Abstract

We prove motivic mirror symmetry for full-rank sink-recurrent cluster varieties. We allow skew-symmetrizable principal parts and invert all frozen variables. The rational geometric motive of such a variety is isomorphic to the additive orbifold motive of the mirror stack of Hu and Shende. This gives comparisons of cohomology, higher Chow groups, and algebraic $K$-theory with rational coefficients. The proof combines quantum deformation with the splitting of these motives into Tate summands. For really full-rank skew-symmetric seeds, a finite \'{e}tale morphism realizes the comparison. We also study this geometric comparison in families over the projective line.

Perfect loops of proper schemes

Alexander B. Ivanov · 2611.12719
abstract: presentablemath.AG
Abstract

We study the perfect loop space $LX$ of a proper scheme $X$ over a local field $F$. We show that if $X$ contains a rational curve, then $LX$ is not ind-representable, even in a very weak sense. We also show that if $X$ admits a proper model $\mathfrak{X}$ over the integers $\mathcal{O}$ of $F$, whose geometric special fiber does not contain rational curves, then $L^+\mathfrak{X} \stackrel{\sim}{\to} LX$ is an isomorphism via the natural map, where $L^+\mathfrak{X}$ denotes the perfect positive loop space (jet scheme) of $\mathfrak{X}$. In particular, $LX$ is a scheme in this case.

Coarse Embeddings Beyond Doubling: Persistence Diagrams and Multiparameter Modules

Sushovan Majhi, Atish Mitra, \v{Z}iga Virk · 2611.12866
math.MGmath.ATcross
Abstract

We study coarse embeddings into Hilbert space and the asymptotic dimension of spaces of persistence diagrams and multiparameter persistence modules. We replace doubling, the usual hypothesis for landmark embeddings, by admissible tent covers of bounded multiplicity at every scale: stacking them across dyadic scales gives an explicit $1$-Lipschitz coarse embedding, with lower gauge $c\sqrt{\tau t}$, or nearly linear for slowly decaying weights, and the same covers bound the asymptotic dimension by the multiplicity. The spaces of persistence diagrams are not doubling but carry such covers, and for diagrams with $n$ points our covers improve the constant of the known landmark embedding by a factor exponential in $n$. Slice by slice, the construction embeds multiparameter modules with boundedly many bars per slice under the matching distance on any finite net of slices, a class that contains the modules with at most $n$ generators. Bounding bars or persistent Betti numbers instead of generators fails: two-parameter modules with one bar on every slice have infinite asymptotic dimension, and neither they nor the modules of bounded support and bounded rank admit a coarse embedding into Hilbert space, under the matching or the interleaving distance.

Compact branch sets and injectivity of proper branched coverings

Deguang Zhong · 2611.11844
math.GTmath.ATmath.CVcross
Abstract

In this paper, we show that a proper continuous open discrete map from the open unit ball $B^n$ onto a domain in $\R^n$ with $n\geq5$ is a homeomorphism whenever its branch set is compact. This answers Vuorinen's question affirmatively in every dimension $n\ge5.$ In dimension four, we also show that, under the additional assumption of quasiregularity, the map is a quasiconformal homeomorphism. Discrete Configuration Spaces of Simplex Skeleta: a Middle-Dimensional Manifold Phenomenon

Emilio J. Gonz\'alez, Jes\'us Gonz\'alez, Mary Carmen P\'erez-Morales · 2611.12847
math.AT
Abstract

For a simple graph $\Gamma$ without loops and an integer $n\geq2$, Abrams showed in his Ph.D. thesis that the discrete configuration space $DConf(\Gamma,n)$ is homeomorphic to a closed $n$-manifold if and only if $n=2$ and $\Gamma$ is $K_5$ or $K_{3,3}$, the two minimal obstructions to graph planarity. We show that this manifold property persists for the infinite family of discrete configuration spaces obtained by replacing the Kuratowski graphs with the $d$-skeleton of the simplex of dimension $2d+2$, thus recovering Abrams' $K_5$ case when $d=1$. Global Green-Schwarz response on quaternionic sphere bundles

Yuqi Li, Hao-Yu Sun · 2611.12810
hep-thmath.ATmath.DGcross
Abstract

Local anomaly polynomials describe local variations, but a normalized Green-Schwarz phase also depends on global background data. We explain this dependence for ten-dimensional three-sphere bundles of quaternionic lines over closed spin seven-manifolds. The backgrounds retain two specified $\mathrm{Spin}(16)$ gauge bundles and an integral differential trivialization on the base, including its boundary identifications. The trivialized class is the sum of half the first Pontryagin class of the tangent bundle and the second Chern class of the quaternionic line's underlying complex bundle. For these backgrounds, the calculation combines a generally nonzero local Green-Schwarz integral with a real eta invariant evaluated by a twelve-dimensional Atiyah-Patodi-Singer filling. Together, these terms determine an integral relative characteristic number; the additive response is one third of that number, modulo integers. When the base's third and fourth rational cohomology groups vanish, the response equals eight times the Crowley-Goette invariant, without assuming that the base is simply connected. A calibration using the actual quaternionic line bundle gives additive value one third on $\mathrm{Sp}(2)$, recovering the known third-root phase. Distinct integral String data on one fixed ten-manifold give all three phase values, showing which global background information the response can detect. The physical identifications retain their prescribed fermion data, while the separate application to integral topological modular forms and vertex operator superalgebras requires compatible integral lifts. The response calculation constructs neither a microscopic realization nor an equivalence of complete theories.

Persistence landscape velocity: Closed formulas and a topological probe of quantum criticality

Francisco Belch\'i, Nahuel Statuto · 2611.11358
math.AT
Abstract

We introduce the landscape velocity $\xi$, a scalar measure of variation of a parameter-dependent persistence landscape. For families of finite barcodes whose bars are all born at the fixed filtration value $0$, we show that $\xi$ is the $L^2$ speed of the landscape curve where the sorted death times are differentiable and derive an exact closed formula that requires no persistence landscape at all: the death times of the barcodes, as functions of the parameter, suffice. For the velocity of the empirical mean of several landscapes we also prove a closed formula which, again, strikingly requires neither the empirical mean landscape nor any of the individual landscapes, only the individual barcodes. As an application, we revisit Olsthoorn's persistent homology analysis of the transverse-field Ising chain. Whereas his approach uses different homology degrees and summaries depending on the boundary conditions, ours uses $H_0$ only and a single rule: the position of the maximum of $\xi$. This rule locates the quantum critical point for both periodic and open boundary conditions, and for both mutual-information and correlator-based dissimilarities. The position of the maximum converges to the critical point faster than all the estimators we can extract from Olsthoorn's work. In the 4 scenarios considered (different boundary conditions and different dissimilarity matrices), the peak of the normalized velocity becomes increasingly pronounced with system size, with observed logarithmic growth. This logarithmic growth is proved for periodic boundaries with correlator dissimilarity under stated hypotheses, identifying landscape velocity as a singular topological response to quantum criticality.
