A short proof of the Segal conjecture for $C_p$

Thomas Nikolaus · 2610.39440
author: Thomas Nikolausabstract: topological hochschild homologymath.ATmath.KT
Abstract

We give a new proof of the generalized Segal conjecture for the cyclic group of prime order p, i.e. the fact that the Tate diagonal of any bounded below spectrum is a p-completion. The idea, inspired by Hahn and Wilson's argument, is to use descent along the equivariant map from the p-fold tensor power of the field with p elements to its topological Hochschild homology. The only computational input that we use is the calculation of the homotopy rings of this topological Hochschild homology and its Tate construction, together with the map induced by the cyclotomic Frobenius.

On equivariant affineness in elliptic cohomology

Jack Morgan Davies, Sil Linskens, Leonard Tokic · 2610.39583
author: Jack Morgan Daviesauthor: Sil Linskenstitle: equivariantabstract: spectral algebraic geometryabstract: ring spectrabstract: infinity-categormath.ATmath.AGmath.KT
Abstract

We investigate equivariant affineness in spectral algebraic geometry: the phenomenon where $\infty$-categories of quasi-coherent sheaves of a spectral stack are equivalent to $\infty$-categories of modules over an equivariant ring spectrum. In the nonequivariant case, this is known as 0-affineness, and has been studied by Gaitsgory, Mathew--Meier, amongst others. Allowing for equivariant phenomena opens up a much wider class of spectral stacks to be studied through the lens of spectra. The spectral stacks at the heart of our analysis are oriented elliptic curves, their associated Hilbert schemes, and related constructions. When working over the universal base, so the moduli stack of oriented elliptic curves, we show that these spectral stacks are $U(1)$-, $U(n)$-, and $SU(n)$-equivariantly affine, with associated $G$-ring spectrum Gepner--Meier's equivariant topological modular forms $\mathrm{TMF}_G$. Our results also encompass the groups $Sp(n)$ as well as all finite products of such groups. Our most general result is a relative statement over an arbitrary base stack. These results can be viewed as an integral and global refinement of Greenlees' algebraic model for modules over rational $U(1)$-equivariant elliptic cohomology.

Permutation twisted cohomology, reduced and immersed

Juan Omar G\'omez, Sam K. Miller · 2610.40602
author: Juan Omar Gómezabstract: balmer spectrmath.RTmath.AGmath.ATmath.CT
Abstract

In this paper, we introduce a `reduced' form of permutation twisted cohomology as introduced by Balmer--Gallauer and further developed by Miller. This ring is bigraded by a well-chosen choice of endotrivial complex, rather than multigraded by a family of endotrivial complexes. The Balmer spectrum of the small derived category of permutation modules openly immerses into the homogeneous spectrum of this ring. We also show that for $p$-groups, Miller's `remixed' twisted cohomology ring also admits the Balmer spectrum as an open subspace, and confirm in both cases it can be upgraded to have Dirac scheme structure; furthermore, we prove that the two open covers witnessing this are in general different.

Discretisation in algebraic $K$-theory of independent ample groupoids and their Steinberg algebras

Guillermo Corti\~nas, Xin Li · 2610.38791
title: algebraic k-theormath.KTmath.OAmath.RA
Abstract

An independent ample groupoid $G$ comes equipped with a discrete groupoid $G_d$ and a proper \'etale correspondence $\Omega:G_d\to G$. Discretisation with respect to a homological invariant $\mathcal{H}$ holds when $\mathcal{H}(\Omega)$ is an equivalence. We show for example that discretisation with respect to the $K$-theory spectrum $\mathbb{K} \mathfrak{B}_G$ of the permutative category of bisections always holds, establish excision and continuity for this invariant, and use all this and explicit resolutions by independent groupoids to compute it for several families of ample groupoids. We also prove discretisation with respect to invariants of the Steinberg algebra $\ell[G]$ such as (homotopy) algebraic $K$-theory in several particular cases, and carry out computations.

G-equivariant Alterfold TQFT in any dimension

Zixuan Feng, Zhengwei Liu · 2610.40041
title: equivariantmath.QAmath-phmath.CTmath.GTcross
Abstract

For a discrete group $G$, we construct an $(n+1)$-dimensional $G$-equivariant Alterfold topological quantum field theory from a $G$-equivariant $S^n$-algebra satisfying strong semisimplicity, complete finiteness, and Morita invariance. We use state sum on triangulation to define a partition function and prove that it is invariant under local surgery relations and is invariant under gauge transformations relative to the boundary. If the input is given a reflection-positive structure, the theory is unitary. For $n=2$ and input reconstructed from a strict faithful spherical $G$-fusion category, the restriction to ordinary $G$-cobordisms is naturally isomorphic to the Turaev--Virelizier state-sum HQFT \cite{turaev20123}. A Tambara-Yamagami example recovers the Quon language \cite{liu2017quon} as a $\mathbb{Z}_2$-equivariant Alterfold TQFT.

On the motivic approach to the Abhyankar rectifiability problem and concrete rectifications

A. S. Frolov, I. S. Dorofeev · 2610.38474
title: motivicmath.AGmath.AT
Abstract

We study closed embeddings of the complex affine line into affine three-space. We prove that, for every such embedding, the complete-intersection normal map identifies the $\mathbb{A}^1$-fundamental sheaf of its complement with $\mathbf{K}_2^{\mathrm{MW}}$. The proof compares torsors under arbitrary strongly $\mathbb{A}^1$-invariant group sheaves. A nodal projection expresses a torsor by a normal parameter and finitely many node parameters. Extension across the node axes gives equations for the latter. Their correction terms increase a nilpotent filtration generated by the Milnor-Witt symbols of the branch-height differences. We deduce the vanishing of the first Alexander sheaf of every complex embedded line. We also construct polynomial rectifications using finite inverses and affine modifications, including rectifications of the Shastri trefoil and a harmonic figure-eight over $\mathbb{Z}[a]/(a^2-a+1)$, with arbitrary base change. The companion repository (https://github.com/qft98romanov-hash/abhyankar-moh-trefoil) contains explicit rectifiers, their inverses, proofs, and symbolic checks. We prove that a normal map with a polynomial section and geometric generic affine-line fiber is a coordinate projection. We formulate the remaining rectifiability and descent questions; the general complex rectifiability problem is not settled here. An appendix treats other fields, normal orientations, and arithmetic descent.

Spectral Structures on Vector Bundles

Nikita Nikolaev · 2610.38972
abstract: presentablemath.AGmath.CV
Abstract

We develop an intrinsic theory of spectral structures on holomorphic vector bundles over compact Riemann surfaces, generalising eigenspace decompositions without eigenvalues. Their geometric realisations are presentations of vector bundles as pushforwards of line bundles along finite branched covers. We construct their analytic moduli stack locally of finite presentation and identify it with the relative Picard stack of the universal family of finite covers. We prove that the forgetful morphism to the stack of vector bundles is representable by analytic spaces and is smooth and surjective on a distinguished smooth open substack. Consequently, every holomorphic family of vector bundles locally admits spectral presentations along smooth connected simply branched covers, and these presentations can be deformed alongside the underlying bundles. The relative Picard description yields explicit local dimension formulas and recovers the stack of regular Higgs bundles as base change along the Hitchin base. We also characterise the additional data supplied to a spectral structure by a regular Higgs field as a global twisted generator, and give examples of smooth connected spectral structures which cannot arise from any Higgs field.

The $6\frac{1}{2}$-functor formalism of Cartier modules

Klaus Mattis, Timo Wei{\ss} · 2610.40200
abstract: presentableabstract: lax limitmath.AGmath.CT
Abstract

Let $p$ be a prime. We construct a coherent formalism of geometric operations on Cartier modules with coefficients in ind-coherent sheaves on noetherian derived $\mathbb F_p$-schemes. For such a scheme $X$, we consider the stable presentable category $\mathrm{Cart}(X)$ of ind-coherent sheaves $M$ equipped with a structural morphism $F_{*,X}M \to M$, where $F_X$ denotes the Frobenius endomorphism. We construct pushforward functors for arbitrary morphisms and exceptional pullback functors for separated morphisms almost of finite type, together with coherent composition and base-change equivalences. We single out conditions under which these functors admit further adjoints. On derived schemes separated and almost of finite type over $\mathbb F_p$, we construct compatible closed symmetric monoidal structures given by shriek tensor products, and establish which functors are symmetric monoidal. The constructions arise from a general procedure for forming functor formalisms of Cartier objects, using their description as lax limits and the universal properties of span $2$-categories developed by Cnossen--Lenz--Linskens [arXiv:2505.19192].

Dongming Zhang · 2610.37885
math.AT
Abstract

We compute the $RO$-graded Tambara functor of $H\underline{\mathbb{Z}}$ for every finite cyclic group. We introduce a valuation language for homotopy groups: for prime-power cyclic group equivariant, classes are represented by compatible vertex arrays or by edge arrays. In these coordinates we compute products, restrictions, transfers, Weyl actions, and norms. Then we assemble the results into arbitrary finite cyclic group equivariant case. Besides the computation, we also develop the Tate compression theory, which is used in the computation. From it we also give a filtration for $\pi_\star(H\underline{\mathbb{Z}})$, compatible with products, restrictions, transfers, Weyl actions, and norms.

Formality transmission

Coline Emprin · 2610.39767
math.ATmath.AGmath.QA
Abstract

Across mathematics, formality results are usually established through general criteria rather than by constructing explicit equivalences. These criteria include domination, puncturing, highly-connected formality, intrinsic formality, formality descent, formality in families, purity, etc. In this article, we prove that all these criteria are instances of a single theorem, formality transmission. Gromov-Hausdorff versus Hausdorff distances for Euclidean submanifolds with positive reach

Prasanna Brahme, William Ott · 2610.40286
math.MGmath.ATcross
Abstract

This paper is about comparing the Gromov-Hausdorff distance between a pair of sets to the Hausdorff distance between the pair. Recent work on this problem has focused on two settings, Riemannian manifolds and metric graphs. Here, we focus on embedded submanifolds of Euclidean space endowed with the Euclidean metric. We prove that when $\mathcal{M}\subset\mathbb{R}^n$ is a compact connected embedded submanifold without boundary, with positive reach, and $\mathrm{A}\subset\mathcal{M}$ is a subset that is sufficiently dense in $\mathcal{M}$ with respect to the reach, the Gromov-Hausdorff distance between $\mathrm{A}$ and $\mathcal{M}$ is bounded below by $1/2$ of the Hausdorff distance between them. We extend this result in two ways. First, we show that the factor $1/2$ can be improved using Jung's theorem. Second, we prove bounds on the Gromov-Hausdorff distance for pairs of subsets of $\mathcal{M}$. When $\mathbb{S}^{1}$ is equipped with the two-dimensional Euclidean metric, the Gromov-Hausdorff distance between $\mathbb{S}^{1}$ and any sufficiently dense subset of $\mathbb{S}^{1}$ can be expressed explicitly as a function of the Hausdorff distance between the two. We use interleavings between geometric simplicial complexes, nerve lemmas, properties of reach and the nearest-point projection map, and the fundamental class to produce the inequalities for general $\mathcal{M}$. We use the Borsuk-Ulam theorem for $\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{S}^1\subset \mathbb{R}^2$.

Homotopy types and torsion in homology of generalized chessboard complexes

Ivan Limonchenko, Seonghyeon Yu · 2610.37827
math.COmath.ATcross
Abstract

Multiple chessboard complexes are deleted joins of skeleta of a simplex. We classify their homotopy types for at most three rows and extend this classification to arbitrary column capacities, with the $2$-torus as the only exception to a wedge of spheres. We determine explicit homotopy decompositions for two families with four and five rows and use a common capacity redistribution map to construct infinite families with $2$- and $3$-torsion in integral homology. Together, these results give a negative answer to the Segre part of the problem posed by Reiner and Roberts on characteristic-free resolutions of Segre and Veronese modules and identify three as the largest number of rows for which all generalized chessboard complexes have torsion-free integral homology. Our $3$-torsion construction applies to every square ordinary chessboard complex with at least five rows. Combined with earlier results, it completes the proof of the Shareshian-Wachs conjecture on torsion in chessboard complexes.

Linking-form splitting and four-dimensional genera of knots

Seong-Jin Lee · 2610.40183
math.GTmath-phmath.ATmath.MPcross
Abstract

By Gilmer's splitting lemma, the linking form of a rational homology sphere that bounds a 4-manifold is the orthogonal sum of a metabolic summand and a summand presented by a form of the rank of the intersection form. The lemma underlies Gilmer's slice genus theorem and the rank criteria of Murakami-Yasuhara and Gilmer-Livingston for nonorientable surfaces and the 4-dimensional clasp number. We show that the lemma and these results, as stated, are false. For each prime $p \equiv 3 \pmod 8$ we construct knots bounding smooth M\"obius bands, and knots bounding smooth punctured tori with clasp number one, whose linking forms have no such summand of rank at most two. We then determine what remains true: the lemma holds up to Witt equivalence and for cube-free orders, and its conclusions about summands hold away from exceptional primes and for orientable surfaces of genus at least two. Finally, we find the optimal Witt-class bound for the topological nonorientable 4-genus.

On a Conjecture of Fiedorowicz

Oren Ben-Bassat · 2610.38760
math.ATmath.GRmath.RA
Abstract

Fiedorowicz conjectured that every finite simply connected CW complex is homotopy equivalent to the classifying space of a finite monoid. We prove this conjecture. Our main construction takes a finite monoid M and a map from a finite connected CW complex into the classifying space of M, and produces a new finite monoid whose classifying space is homotopy equivalent to the mapping cone of the given map. Applying this construction successively to the attaching maps of a finite CW model proves the result. As an explicit example of the algorithm, we construct a 1795-element monoid whose classifying space is homotopy equivalent to two dimensional complex projective space.

math.ATmath.FA
Abstract

Given an orientable rank-$d$ vector bundle over a $d$-manifold, the Euler class is the unique obstruction to finding a nowhere-zero section. An equal norm Parseval frame is a certain collection of sections, one of whose properties is that the lengths of each vector at each point are equal. This makes the Euler class an obvious obstruction to finding such a frame. In this work we show that this is the only obstruction so long as the number of sections is at least $2d+2$. We also identify precise obstructions when the number of sections is $2d$ and produce examples showing that for smaller numbers of sections it is sometimes impossible to find Parseval frame fields.

The geometry of Floquet states

math-phmath.ATmath.DGmath.MPcross
Abstract

This paper studies the moduli space of "good" periodic Hamiltonians, i.e., periodic Hamiltonians with nondegenerate Floquet spectrum. The corresponding Floquet states determine a geometric model for the moduli space of such Hamiltonians. The model is expressed in terms of configuration spaces, flag manifolds, and principal bundles. A corollary of the main result is the complete determination of the rational cohomology ring of the space of good periodic Hamiltonians. The paper also considers the adiabatic driving of a 1-parameter family of good periodic Hamiltonians. We formulate two conjectures describing the resulting asymptotic quantum evolution. These are Floquet analogues of the adiabatic theorem in quantum mechanics.

The Segal conjecture for large objects and semiadditive lifts

Vignesh Subramanian · 2610.40209
math.AT
Abstract

We prove a projection formula for homotopy fixed points of trivial actions of finite $p$-groups. More precisely, for every bounded-below, $p$-complete spectrum $X$ whose mod-$p$ homology is $I$-complete, the canonical map \[ (\mathbb S_p)^{BG}\otimes X \overset{\simeq}{\rightarrow} X^{BG} \] is an equivalence for every finite $p$-group $G$, where $I$ is the augmentation ideal of the Steenrod algebra.
