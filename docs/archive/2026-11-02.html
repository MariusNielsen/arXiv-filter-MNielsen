Lax-idempotent monads in homotopy theory

Fernando Abell\'an, Thomas Blom · 2610.41904
author (watch): Fernando Abellánabstract: infinity-operadabstract: (infinity,2)abstract: presentablemath.CT
Abstract

We extend the theory of lax-idempotent monads to the setting of $(\infty,2)$-categories. In particular, we prove an analogue of Kock and Z\"oberlein's characterization of the category of algebras over a lax-idempotent monad. We then apply this theory to many examples that arise naturally in homotopy theory. For example, we show that $(\infty,1)$-toposes are monadic over presentable $(\infty,1)$-categories, with left adjoint given by the symmetric $(\infty,1)$-topos. We also show that $\infty$-operads are comonadic over symmetric monoidal $(\infty,1)$-categories through the monoidal envelope construction, obtaining along the way a new proof of Barkan-Steinebrunner's result that the envelope functor is a faithful inclusion. We furthermore use this theory to simplify Ramzi's proof that dualizable stable $(\infty,1)$-categories are comonadic over $\aleph_1$-compactly generated stable $(\infty,1)$-categories.

Defect TQFTs, the Generalized Cobordism Hypothesis, and Algebraic $K$-Theory

Keyao Peng · 2610.41105
title: algebraic k-theorabstract: higher algebraabstract: dualizable categormath.KTmath.AT
Abstract

By constructing a fully dualizable category $\mathrm{Bord}^{\mathcal{C}}_n$ as the left adjoint of the forgetful functor, we state a generalized cobordism hypothesis. As a corollary, we establish a generalized Pontryagin-Thom isomorphism that gives a geometric interpretation of higher algebraic $K$-theory using cobordism classes of framed manifolds and stratified spaces decorated by categorical string diagrams, which we call lasagnas. For finite-dimensional vector spaces over a field, these decorations are branched local systems. We also examine low-dimensional representatives, including $K_1$, Steinberg-group presentations of $K_2$, and fillings related to the Steinberg relation, providing a more explicit construction of Ghys' manifold.

Deformations of Calabi--Yau varieties over transversal or crystalline prisms

Tingxiao Shi · 2610.41727
title: prismsabstract: prismaticmath.AG
Abstract

Recent work of Brantner and Taelman shows that derived deformation theory and period maps can recover strong mixed-characteristic lifting results. In this paper, we develop a prismatic version of this method for Calabi--Yau varieties over transversal or crystalline prisms, constructing compatible deformations over $A/I^n$ for every $n$ under suitable hypotheses. We also apply our method to $F$-split Calabi--Yau varieties over a field $k$ of characteristic $p>2$. In particular, we consider an $F$-split smooth proper variety $X$ of dimension $d$ over an algebraically closed field $k$ of characteristic $p>2$, with $H^{d-1}(X,O_X)=0$ and trivial canonical bundle. We show that $X$ admits a lift to $W_3(k)$.

Descent and Decomposability of Algebras of Exponent 2 in characteristic 2

Anne Qu\'eguiner-Mathieu, Jean-Pierre Tignol · 2610.40673
title: descentmath.RAmath.KTmath.NTcross
Abstract

We define an invariant of central simple algebras of degree 8 and exponent 2 over fields of characteristic 2. It is built from the discriminant Pfister forms of symplectic involutions, takes values in a quotient of the third part of the graded group associated to the fundamental filtration of the Witt group of quadratic forms, and vanishes on tensor products of three quaternion algebras, thus giving a necessary condition for decomposability. Variants of the consctuction yield necessary and sufficient conditions for a biquaternion algebra to descend from a quadratic extension of the base field, and for a quaternion algebra to descent from a biquadratic extension that is not purely inseparable. Our results extend known results both in characteristic 2 and in characteristic different from 2, see the introduction. Using residue maps for quadratic forms, we show that these invariants do not vanish in general, and we give an explicit central simple algebra of degree 8 and exponent 2 with nonzero invariant, which is therefore indecomposable. As explained in the appendix, the purely inseparable case is quite different; in particular, a quaternion algebra descents if and only if its Brauer class also does.

Homotopy in enriched functor categories: Frobenius exact structures and weak Serre functors

Sinem Odabasi · 2610.40746
title: t structuremath.CTmath.KT
Abstract

Let $(\V, \otimes, I,[-,-])$ be a closed symmetric monoidal category with finite (co)limits. Assume that $\V$ is weakly idempotent complete and additive. For a given small $\V$-category $\A$, we investigate sufficient conditions on $\A$ under which the ordinary category $[\A, \V]_0$ of $\V$-functors from $\A$ to $\V$, equipped with the exact structure $\mathcal{E}_{\textrm{lowt}}$, is a Frobenius exact category,and hence, induces a triangulated stable category. The exact structure $\mathcal{E}_{\textrm{lowt}}$ encompasses the degre-wise trivial exact structure on categories of $N$-chain complexes and $m$-periodic chain complexes, with terms in a variety of settings, including left $R$-modules, quasi-coherent sheaves on a scheme, and Banach spaces. We subsequently address the question of describing abstract left and right homotopy relations in $([\A, \V]_0;\mathcal{E}_{\textrm{lowt}})$ in terms of families of morphisms defined objectwise, as in the cases of chain-homotopy and relative homotopy relations. To this end, we introduce the notion of left/right-weak Serre $\V$-functors on $\A$, and investigate their relationships with the classical Serre functor relative to a commutative ring $k$.

We prove that birational projective irreducible holomorphic symplectic manifolds have equivalent integral cohomological motives as commutative algebra objects in $\mathrm{SH}(\mathbb C)$. Tate and Artin--Tate Motivic Tensor-Triangular Geometry

Jean Paul Schemeil, Fraser Sparks · 2610.40806
title: tensor-triangulartitle: motivicabstract: motivic cohomologabstract: balmer spectrmath.AGmath.ATmath.CTmath.KT
Abstract

We compute the Balmer spectra of geometric Tate and Artin--Tate motives with $\mathbb{F}_p$-coefficients over any field whose characteristic differs from $p$. For Tate motives, we find the bigraded comparison map to be a homeomorphism to the homogeneous spectrum of the motivic cohomology of the base field. For Artin--Tate motives, we describe the spectrum as a gluing of the spectrum of Artin motives computed by Balmer--Gallauer with the punctured Tate spectrum. We determine all specialisations between these pieces and the resulting topology. Under the hypothesis that the spectrum of rational Tate motives is a singleton, we also compute the integral Tate spectrum after inverting the exponential characteristic of the base field. As a consequence of our results, we identify the punctured Tate spectrum as an open subspace of the spectrum of the category of geometric Voevodsky motives, and draw some conclusions concerning the latter. Finally, we include an appendix where we define generalised periodicity in the context of tensor-triangular geometry, and explore its applications in motivic settings.

Gersten injectivity and Morel's purity conjecture

Niels FELD · 2611.00017
abstract: algebraic k-theormath.AGmath.KTHexagonAI-generated (no human author claimed)
Abstract

Morel conjectured that the $\mathbb{A}^1$-homotopy sheaves of an $S^1$-spectrum built from suspension spectra of smooth projective schemes are pure; one condition in purity is injectivity of restriction to dense open subschemes. Over a regular base $S$, we show that this injectivity condition, for the homotopy sheaves of algebraic $K$-theory with coefficients in an arbitrary spectrum, is equivalent to Gersten injectivity at the henselizations of all local rings of smooth $S$-schemes; and that these $K$-theory spectra are pure in Morel's sense. Hence every henselian regular local ring at which Gersten injectivity fails, integrally, rationally, or with finite coefficients, gives a counterexample to Morel's conjecture. The author's finite-coefficient counterexample over $\mathbb{Z}_3[[x,y]]/(3+x^2-y^3)$ therefore disproves the conjecture, already over the regular scheme $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}[1/2][x,y]/(3+x^2-y^3)$. Recently announced integral and rational Gersten counterexamples, including unpublished work of the author, would show, if confirmed and if they persist after henselization, that the conjecture fails already for the $K$-theory spectrum itself.

Initial Subcategories via Free Presentations of the Constant Functor

Thomas Br\"ustle, Justin Desrochers, Samuel Leblanc · 2610.41068
abstract: presentablemath.CTmath.RT
Abstract

Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a small category. We characterize the full initial subcategories of $\mathcal{C}$ as precisely those whose representables give a free presentation of the constant functor $\Delta_{\mathcal{C}}\mathbb{K}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathrm{Vect}$. Such a presentation yields a formula for the limit of any functor $\mathcal{C} \to \mathrm{Vect}$ as the kernel of a morphism between products of evaluations. When the linearized endomorphism ring of every object is local, we show that a minimal free presentation, whenever one exists, determines the unique minimal full initial subcategory, up to equivalence. Bass's characterization of perfect rings gives a sufficient condition for the existence of such presentations. In particular, every finite category with no nonidentity endomorphisms, such as a finite poset, contains a unique minimal initial full subcategory. In the poset case, our results recover the minimal initial subposet constructed in [BDL27] and [DL26]. When $\mathcal{C}$ is the free category on a finite quiver, we give an explicit construction of its minimal initial full subcategories, show that every such subcategory arises from this construction, and provide algorithms with time-complexity bounds.

The coanalyticity of the reduced K-theory on modules over free tensor algebras of dualizable t-categories

Li He · 2610.41566
abstract: infinity-categorabstract: goodwilliemath.KTmath.CT
Abstract

In this paper, we formulate Land-Tamme's $\boxtimes$-construction and $\odot$-construction for continuous exact monads on dualizable stable $\infty$-categories, and establish the behaviour of localizing invariants on pushouts of left modules over continuous exact monads on dualizable stable $\infty$-categories. Combining the Land-Tamme theory of continuous exact monads with the theory of analytic functors in Goodwillie calculus, we show that the reduced K-theory on modules over free tensor algebras of dualizable t-categories is modified 1-coanalytic or modified 2-coanalytic, depending on the coconnectivity of the base algebras. In particular, this answers Efimov's question concerning coanalyticity in the affirmative.

An odd version of the Grothendieck--Teichm\"uller Lie algebra and the odd Deligne--Drinfeld conjecture

Florian Naef, Thomas Willwacher · 2610.41766
math.QAmath.ATcross
Abstract

We study an ``odd'' version of the Grothendieck--Teichm\"uller Lie algebra that is obtained by replacing free Lie algebras in even generators by free graded Lie algebras in odd generators in the classical definition. These odd variants are related to the ``odd'' Kontsevich graph complex in the same way as the classical Grothendieck--Teichm\"uller group is related to the even Kontsevich graph complex. We show an odd analog of the Deligne--Drinfeld conjecture, namely that the odd Grothendieck--Teichm\"uller Lie algebra is isomorphic to a free graded Lie algebra in one odd generator of weight 3. Thus we in particular compute the odd graph cohomology in the bottom cohomological degree, showing that it is mostly zero.

Higher limits over the fusion orbit category via subgroup complexes

Ergun Yalcin · 2610.40915
math.AT
Abstract

Let $\mathcal{F}$ be a saturated fusion system over a finite $p$-group $S$, and $\mathcal{O}^c (\mathcal{F})$ denote the centric orbit category of $\mathcal{F}$. Suppose that $G$ is a discrete group realizing $\mathcal{F}$, and $\mathcal{C}$ is the collection of all $p$-centric subgroups in $G$. We prove that for every $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)} \mathcal{O} ^c (\mathcal{F})$-module $M$, the higher limits $\lim ^* _{\mathcal{O}^c (\mathcal{F})} M$ are isomorphic to the equivariant cohomology $H_ G ^* (|\mathcal{C}| ; \mathcal{M} )$, where $\mathcal{M}$ is the $G$-equivariant coefficient system on $|\mathcal{C}|$ induced by $M$. This is the fusion system version of a theorem by Grodal for higher limits over the $p$-orbit category $\mathcal{O}_p(G)$ of a finite group $G$. As a consequence, for every fusion system $\mathcal{F}$, we obtain a finite sequence of cohomology groups, involving the cohomology of $\mathcal{F}$, which is exact if and only if the mod-$p$ subgroup decomposition for $\mathcal{F}$ over $\mathcal{F}$-centric subgroups is sharp.

Landscape Intervals: A Geometric Framework for Multiparameter Persistence Landscapes

Pan Fang, Washington Mio, Tom Needham · 2610.42090
math.AT
Abstract

Classical persistent homology provides a summary of the multiscale topology of a filtration of a space. These summaries are not naturally valued in a vector space, limiting their direct use in standard machine learning pipelines. To address this issue, Bubenik introduced persistence landscapes, vector-valued invariants of persistence modules, which operate by conveniently encoding the behavior of rank functions of modules along the parameter axis. When passing to multiparameter persistent homology, changes in the rank functions vary by different amounts in different directions. In this paper, we introduce the landscape interval, a subset of the parameter space of a multiparameter persistence module which records changes in the rank function in all directions simultaneously. This geometric perspective leads to two novel invariants which serve as notions of persistence landscapes for multiparameter modules. We show that our invariants generalize existing landscape-type invariants: in particular, they recover Bubenik's landscapes in the one-parameter setting and allow us to obtain the multiparameter landscapes introduced by Vipond. For our main theoretical contributions, we establish two exact identities connecting landscapes with erosion distances, prove that our invariants are stable under perturbations, and refine the interleaving-stability bounds for the existing landscape-type invariants. We also show that on a certain class of multiparameter persistence modules, the full invariants can be recovered from finite information about the module. On the practical side, we introduce a robust numerical framework for extracting our invariants from real data and for incorporating them into standard machine learning pipelines. The framework is illustrated on surface mesh and medical image data.

Odd-Degree Rigidity and a Numerical Characterization of Complete Flag Varieties

Kiwamu Watanabe · 2610.41237
math.AGmath.ATmath.CVmath.DG
Abstract

Let $F=G/B$ be the complete flag variety of a connected semisimple complex linear algebraic group. We prove that if a smooth Fano variety $X$ has the same dimension and Picard number as $F$, then the existence of a continuous map $X\to F$ of odd topological degree implies $X$ is isomorphic to $F$. In particular, a smooth Fano variety homotopy equivalent to $F$ is isomorphic to $F$. The algebraic input is a numerical characterization of complete flags: a positive-dimensional smooth Fano variety $X$ is a complete flag variety if and only if its pseudoindex $\iota_X$ is at least two and ${\rm ker}({\rm Sym}^2N^1(X)_{\mathbb R}\to N^2(X)_{\mathbb R})$ contains a positive-definite quadratic form. The pseudoindex condition can equivalently be replaced by the divisibility of $-K_X$ by two in ${\rm Pic}(X)$. The key observation is that a single definite quadratic relation excludes surfaces in every fiber of any elementary contraction; standard contraction theorems then yield smooth ${\mathbb P}^1$-fibrations and the characterization of complete flags applies. For the odd-degree theorem, Poincar\'e duality and a mod-two Wu-class argument show that the anticanonical class is divisible by two; comparison of the integral coroot lattices then identifies the flag. Most proofs were developed through extensive discussions with OpenAI's ChatGPT (GPT-5.6 Sol and GPT-6), based on mathematical directions supplied by the author and arguments suggested by ChatGPT; see the AI Disclosure.
