Infinity operad pairs, higher distributivity and spectral Tambara functors

Aowen Fan · 2611.09115
title: infinity operadabstract: ring spectrabstract: e_infinity-ringmath.ATmath.CT
Abstract

We introduce an $\infty$-categorical model of operad pairs and their associated bimonoidal categories. We study the related concepts such as cocartesian-cartesian operad pairs, algebra pairs and the Day convolution. Using the Day convolution for operad pairs, we can define distributive functors between a symmetric bimonoidal category and a cartesian closed monoidal category. As an application, we show that for a finite group $G$, the operadic distributive functors from $\mathrm{Span}(\mathsf{Fin}_G)$ to any cartesian closed monoidal category coincides with the distributive functors defined by the distributive diagram. Therefore, we show that the connective $G$-equivariant $\mathbb{E}_{\infty}$-ring spectra are equivalently the spectral $G$-Tambara functors.

The splitting of the stable space of equivariant $h$-cobordisms

Thomas Goodwillie, Kiyoshi Igusa, Cary Malkiewich, Mona Merling · 2611.09557
title: equivariantmath.ATmath.GTmath.KT
Abstract

We prove that the space of equivariant $h$-cobordisms on a smooth $G$-manifold $M$ has a tom Dieck style splitting, after stabilization. As an intermediate step, we introduce a space of isovariant $h$-cobordisms on $M$ and prove that this space also has a splitting, even before stabilizing. This completes the proof of the equivariant version of Waldhausen's stable parametrized $h$-cobordism theorem. Along the way, we establish several foundational results on equivariant smooth manifolds with corners.

Two-step nilpotent varieties as categories of modules over a square ring

Logan Geenis, Manfred Hartl, Tim Van der Linden · 2611.09465
title: nilpotenmath.CTmath.RA
Abstract

We characterise the two-step nilpotent varieties of algebras: the semi-abelian varieties in which all ternary cosmash products vanish. Just as abelian varieties are the categories of modules over a ring, two-step nilpotent varieties are exactly the categories of modules over a square ring in the sense of Baues, Hartl and Pirashvili. We construct the square ring explicitly from the full subcategory on the free algebras on one and two generators, and give complete proofs. As consequences we obtain a description of any two-step nilpotent variety by generators and relations, an intrinsic characterisation in terms of quadratic term operations, and an account of its abelian core and of the lower central series of its objects in terms of the square ring. Along the way, we associate with any commutative ring $R$ a square ring whose modules, the two-step nilpotent $R$-groups, extend Hall's nilpotent $R$-groups of class two from $2$-binomial rings to arbitrary rings of coefficients.

An Explicit Equation for the $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{C})$ Character Variety of $\mathbb{RP}^2 \# \mathbb{RP}^2 \# \mathbb{RP}^2$

Yinxi Chen · 2611.08513
math.AGmath.AT
Abstract

Let $N$ denote the closed non-orientable surface of Euler characteristic $-1$, i.e. the connected sum of three real projective planes $N = \mathbb{RP}^2 \# \mathbb{RP}^2 \# \mathbb{RP}^2$, with fundamental group presented as $\langle A,B,C \mid A^2 B^2 C^2 = I \rangle$ via the standard symmetric cross-cap model. Building on the classical trace-coordinate methods of Fricke, Vogt, and Goldman for free groups, and on the qualitative study of representation varieties of non-orientable surfaces by Benyash-Krivets and Chernousov, we give an explicit affine equation for a dense open piece of the $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{C})$ GIT character variety $X(N)$. We prove that the trace coordinates $a = \mathrm{tr}(A)$, $b = \mathrm{tr}(B)$, $c = \mathrm{tr}(C)$, $p = \mathrm{tr}(AB)$ satisfy the polynomial relation $c^2 = abp - a^2 - b^2 + 4$, that this hypersurface is smooth and irreducible, and that on the open locus $c \neq 0$ it is isomorphic to the image of $X(N)$ under the trace map, with the remaining trace coordinates $\mathrm{tr}(BC)$, $\mathrm{tr}(CA)$, $\mathrm{tr}(ABC)$ recovered as regular functions on this locus. We further identify the boundary locus $c = 0$ with representations satisfying the matrix identity $A^2 B^2 = -I$, and describe its representation-theoretic structure, while leaving a complete global classification along this locus as an open problem. All key trace identities are proved from Cayley-Hamilton first principles and independently checked numerically against explicit matrices.

Beyond the Lower-Star Filtration: Alternative Filtration Methods for Persistent Homology

Innocent Abaa, Umar Islambekov · 2611.08833
stat.MLcs.LGmath.ATstat.APcross
Abstract

In the lower-star filtration (LSF), simplices are assigned filtration values according to the maximum-vertex rule, with each simplex entering the filtration when its highest-valued vertex appears. A limitation of this construction is that some higher-dimensional simplices may enter simultaneously with their boundary faces, causing certain topological features to be filled immediately upon formation and thus not appear in the resulting persistence diagram. To address this limitation, we propose three alternative filtration methods: the \(\varepsilon\)-lower-star filtration (ELSF), the \(L_p\) filtration (LpF), and the softmax filtration (SMF). {ELSF introduces a dimension-dependent delay to the maximum-vertex rule,} allowing higher-dimensional simplices to enter the filtration after their boundary faces, whereas {LpF and SMF use aggregation functions that depend on all vertex values rather} than solely on their maximum. By modifying how filtration values are assigned, these methods allow simplices to enter the filtration more progressively, so that topological features such as loops and voids may persist over longer filtration intervals rather than being filled immediately. Consequently, the resulting persistence diagrams may include persistence features that are not observed under LSF, potentially providing useful information for distinguishing data populations. Experimental results on simulated and real-world datasets demonstrate that the proposed filtration methods are computationally efficient and can provide greater sensitivity to structural differences in the data than LSF.

Directed systems of A-spaces and weak cores

Jonathan Ariel Barmak, Juan Galotto · 2611.09886
math.AT
Abstract

We prove that for any regular CW-complex $X$ there exists a quotient map $X\to Y$ that is a weak homotopy equivalence and which is minimal with that property, meaning that any quotient $Y\to Z$ that is a weak equivalence, must be a homeomorphism. The space $Y$ we obtain is an Alexandroff space: a space in which arbitrary intersections of open sets are open. The weak core $Y$ of $X$ is not unique in general. We study homotopy invariants preserved by filtered colimits of Alexandroff spaces. This can be seen as an analogous to Stong's theory of cores of finite topological spaces.

How many points does it take to see the homotopy of a space? Minimal acyclic categorical models

Isaac Carcac\'ia-Campos · 2611.09718
math.ATmath.CT
Abstract

Every connected homotopy type is the classifying space of a small category, but this general result gives little control over the categories involved. We study finite acyclic categorical models and measure their complexity by the number of objects. Our main tool is the sink lemma, which describes the addition of a sink as the attachment of a cone along an explicit map. Using it, we determine the minimum number of objects required to model spheres, bouquets of spheres, Moore spaces of cyclic groups, real projective spaces, and lens spaces. We also exhibit infinitely many pairwise non-isomorphic rigid minimal models of the sphere. To include spaces admitting no finite model, we introduce a truncated invariant measuring the minimum number of objects required for a finite acyclic category to approximate a space through degree n. Spreading a finite monoid over n+1 objects produces such a category with an n-connected map to the monoid's classifying space. For every nontrivial finite group, this bound is optimal, although its classifying space admits no finite acyclic model.

New persistent chain spaces: identifying and computing the relevant persistent Laplacians of a filtration

Joris Kirchner, Martina Vittorietti · 2611.09610
math.ATcs.CGmath.COmath.SP
Abstract

Persistent homology (PH) summarizes the topological features of data that persist across scales, but it is insensitive to many geometric properties. Persistent Laplacians (PLs) address this limitation: for a pair of simplicial complexes in a filtration, the kernel of the persistent Laplacian recovers the persistent Betti number, while its non-zero spectrum carries additional geometric information. However, PLs are defined for individual pairs of filtration values, so analysing a whole filtration requires evaluating many operators that largely contain the same information, and the result is hard to summarize and interpret. In this paper, we show that this redundancy has a precise structure. For each pair of filtration values, we decompose the space of chains underlying the persistent up-Laplacian into the contributions inherited from the neighboring pairs and a remaining part, which we call the new persistent chain space. We show that the new persistent chain spaces of all earlier pairs form a direct sum decomposition of this space, so that every persistent up-Laplacian in the filtration is determined by them. Moreover, the new persistent chain spaces are non-trivial only at pairs given by the persistence pairing in the same and in the next homological dimension, so that the persistence pairing determines exactly where the persistent up-Laplacian can change. Experiments on several types of point clouds show that evaluating persistent Laplacians only at these pairs reduces the computation time, both for our implementation and for existing software.
