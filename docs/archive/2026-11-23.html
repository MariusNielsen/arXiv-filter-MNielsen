On the chromatic splitting conjecture in coheight $1$

Tobias Barthel, Lucas Mann, Rin Ray, Tomer M. Schlank, Andrew Senger, Jared Weinstein, Xinyu Zhou · 2611.27879
author: Tobias Barthelauthor: Tomer Schlankauthor: Andrew Sengertitle: chromatic splittingabstract: k(h-1)-localabstract: lubin-tateabstract: e_infinity-ringmath.ATmath.AGmath.NT
Abstract

This article confirms predictions made by Hopkins' chromatic splitting conjecture in stable homotopy theory. A consequence of our main theorem is that a certain map of $K(h-1)$-local $\mathbb{E}_\infty$-rings $\mathbb{S}_{K(h-1)}^{B\mathbb{Z}}\to L_{K(h-1)}\mathbb{S}_{K(h)}$ admits a retract, so long as $p-1$ does not divide $h-1$. If $h=2$ and $p$ is odd, we show this map is an isomorphism. We use methods from the theory of $p$-divisible groups and $p$-adic geometry. Along the way we prove an entirely new kind of "two-tower isomorphism," in analogy with Faltings' isomorphism between the Lubin--Tate and Drinfeld towers, which parametrizes $p$-divisible groups over characteristic $p$ perfectoid spaces with fixed generic fiber in terms of negative Banach--Colmez spaces. Using the two-tower isomorphism, we gain control over the continuous cohomology of the Morava stabilizer group $\mathbb{G}_h$ with coefficients in $\pi_*L_{K(h-1)}E_h$. To study the continuous action of $\mathbb{G}_h$ on $L_{K(h-1)} E_h$, it was necessary to develop the theory of solid Lubin--Tate spectra. Adapting Lurie's constructions, we attach a solid Lubin--Tate spectrum to $p$-divisible groups over a broad class of solid rings in characteristic $p$.

Bridging Badiou's Gaps: Topos Theory and the Bodies of Mathematical Expression

Antti Veilahti · 2611.27280
title: toposabstract: topoimath.CTmath.HOmath.LO
Abstract

This article develops a second stage of an argument begun in 2013, revisiting Alain Badiou's use of topos theory in Being and Event and Logics of Worlds. The previous article diagnosed Badiou's reduction of topos theory to its atomistic, localic form as the scientific scapegoat in his attack on 'democratic materialism'. Now I ask what follows when this logicist illusion is dispelled and the categorical alternative is taken seriously, particularly with respect to forcing, semantic expression, and mathematical subjectification that constitute the key elements of Badiou's earlier work. I thus reconstruct Badiou's own arguments in a corrected form, while transferring them to a categorical framework. The aim is not to introduce new mathematical results, but to identify what is conceptually lost through their reduction to set-theoretic formalism. In particular, I describe Cohen's forcing argument in the context of Grothendieck topoi and Kripke-Joyal semantics, thereby explicating the geometric structures implicit in Badiou's thinking. This reconstruction further brings into view a different way of understanding Badiou's concepts of subject, body, and change. Rather than treating a topos as merely a formal container for mathematical objects, I propose reading it as a body of mathematical expression, aligning with Johnstone's characterization of a topos as a 'universe of discourse'. At the same time, the geometrization of logic allows Badiou's own four-fold typology of change - when applied to topos theory itself as a mathematical 'event' - to be anchored to various historical definitions of a topos. In this way, the article bridges Badiou's two major works while clarifying how his own limited expression of mathematical formalism embodies his 'dialectical', ultimately singular concept of the event, and the way it clouds his philosophical understanding of locality and temporality.

Kneser-Tits over fields of cohomological dimension $\le 2$ (with an appendix by Alexis Bouthier and Federico Scavia)

Kestutis Cesnavicius · 2611.27803
abstract: syntomicmath.AGmath.NT
Abstract

We settle the Kneser-Tits problem over every field $k$ of (separable) cohomological dimension $\le 2$, thus resolving a conjecture of Gille. More precisely, for such a $k$ and a semisimple, simply connected $k$-group $G$ whose indecomposable factors over $k$ are isotropic, we show that $G(k)$ is generated by "elementary matrices" (the $k$-points of the unipotent radicals of the parabolic $k$-subgroups of $G$), in other words, that $K_1^G(k) \cong 1$. In fact, we show this vanishing already when $k$ has separable $p$-dimension $\le 2$ in the sense of Gille with respect to a subset of the torsion primes $p$ of $G$, and we reinterpret the separable $p$-dimension itself via syntomic cohomology. To approach those $G$ that escape the net of previously known cases, for an inclusion of reductive $k$-groups $M \subset L$, we develop a new method based on the Rost invariant for transferring $R$-equivalence on $L/M$ to $R$-equivalence on $L$. To find homogeneous spaces to which our method fruitfully applies, we use the theories of prehomogeneous vector spaces and of special symplectic representations: concretely, for a $32$-dimensional half-spin representation $V$, we argue that the vectors in its unique open orbit $O \subset V$ admit unique up to interchange decompositions into sums of two pure spinors, and show that various double covers of $O$ built from such decompositions are rational varieties over any base field.

Persistent Mayer Path Homology and Mayer Path Laplacian

Dilan Karaguler, Guowei Wei · 2611.26982
math.AT
Abstract

In topological data analysis, path homology accounts for directed graph while combinatorial Laplacian extends homology groups to spectral theory. However, these approaches are built on a boundary operator with condition $\partial^2=0$. Mayer homology replaces this condition with $N$-nilpotent boundary map $\partial^N=0$. By using the primitive $N$-th root of unity in the boundary operator, it relates each degree $n$ to the degrees $n-q$ and $n+N-q$. This work introduces persistent Mayer path homology. We show that Mayer path homology is functorial for strict digraph maps, which gives persistent Mayer path homology for filtered digraphs, and prove that its barcodes are stable under perturbations. For $N=3$ we derive formulas that reduce $\beta_0^{3,1}$ and $\beta_2^{3,2}$ to the classes of vertices identified by squares and the rank of a small matrix indexed by triangles. We also introduce the Mayer path Laplacian and its persistence theory, prove that their kernels recover (persistent) Mayer path homology, show that they distinguish digraphs with the same Mayer path Betti numbers, and prove a stability bound for the eigenvalues of the persistent up-Laplacian. We demonstrate that persistent Mayer path homology detects the formation of feed-forward loops, whose formation is not detected by ordinary path homology. In an application to cucurbit[7]uril, filtered by an interatomic distance normalized by van der Waals radii, Mayer path homology separates contacts that only close cycles from contacts that close feed-forward triangles, and changes at almost four times as many filtration values as path homology after the bonded framework is formed.

Representations of spectral systems over finite distributive lattices

Robert Vandervorst · 2611.27908
math.AT
Abstract

A representation theorem is proved for spectral systems indexed by the lattice of down-sets of a finite poset P. Over an arbitrary associative ring, every excisive, homotopy-exact chain system of differential modules is isomorphic to the chain system of a projective P-graded differential module with a single upper-triangular differential. Consequently, every Cartan-Eilenberg system that is excisive and chain generated, together with its spectral system, admits such a differential model. An example over Z/4 shows that chain generation cannot be dropped. A chain system over Z/8 with short exact rows, whose maps commute only up to homotopy, shows that homotopy-exactness cannot be weakened to distinguished triangles in the derived category. Under finiteness conditions the model is finitely generated. The proof combines a derived horseshoe lemma with induction over the lattice, and yields a short proof and extension of Franzosa's connection matrix theory.
